# What distinguishes the profitable minority of retail derivatives traders, and which measurable safeguards help a small retail options buyer

Scope: NSE index/stock option buying, Rs 10k-25k per trade, intraday to 2-day holds, rule-based + AI assistant (paper first). Research date: October 2026. Practitioner rules of thumb are labelled **[practitioner]**; inferences are labelled as such.

## 1. What SEBI and academic studies say about the minority who profit in Indian F&O

### Takeaway
Across every SEBI study (FY22 through FY26), roughly 88-93% of individual F&O traders lose money after costs. Pure option buyers, who are 93% of traders, lose at a ~90% rate. Persistent profitability is extremely rare: only 0.5% of traders active over FY22-FY26 profited in all five years. Loss rates rise, not fall, with years of experience. The traders who do profit at scale are mostly proprietary desks and FPIs using algorithms, not individuals.

### Cited Findings
**SEBI study, FY25-FY26 (published August 2026; figures via press and forum summaries, primary PDF not read directly)**
- 87.7% of individual traders had net losses in FY26. Aggregate individual net loss was Rs 91,685 crore, down ~18% from Rs 1,11,788 crore in FY25. Average loss per loss-maker was Rs 1.47 lakh; average profit per profit-maker was Rs 1.22 lakh. About 23% of traders accounted for ~90% of losses. — [Business Today, 21 Aug 2026](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/story/rs91685-cr-lost-88-of-individual-traders-lost-money-in-fy26-options-drove-92-of-losses-550474-2026-08-21)
- Options were 92% of aggregate individual losses. 87.7% of options traders lost money versus ~66% of futures traders. — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/story/rs91685-cr-lost-88-of-individual-traders-lost-money-in-fy26-options-drove-92-of-losses-550474-2026-08-21)
- Transaction costs were ~Rs 24,800 crore in each of FY25 and FY26, or Rs 31,628 per trader in FY26 (up from Rs 26,027). The split was brokerage 44%, STT 27%, exchange charges 16%, GST 11%. Costs equalled 35% of loss-makers' gross losses and 21% of profit-makers' gross profits. — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/story/rs91685-cr-lost-88-of-individual-traders-lost-money-in-fy26-options-drove-92-of-losses-550474-2026-08-21)
- Age: traders under 30 were 43% of individuals, and 89% of them lost money. Among traders over 60, 81% lost money. — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/story/rs91685-cr-lost-88-of-individual-traders-lost-money-in-fy26-options-drove-92-of-losses-550474-2026-08-21)
- Expiry concentration of index-option turnover: 59% on expiry day (0DTE) in FY26 (70% in FY25); 75% within 1 day of expiry; 97% within 1 week. Contracts with more than 7 days to expiry were only 3% of FY26 turnover. — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/story/rs91685-cr-lost-88-of-individual-traders-lost-money-in-fy26-options-drove-92-of-losses-550474-2026-08-21)
- Buyers vs sellers (FY26, from a random sample of 5,050 traders, so small categories are indicative only):
  - "Only option buyers" were ~93% of traders, ~90% of them were loss-makers, and loss-makers' average loss was ~Rs 1.3 lakh.
  - "Majorly option sellers" were ~2% of traders, ~44% of them were loss-makers, and loss-makers' average loss was ~Rs 51.7 lakh, a much larger tail.
  — [Zerodha TradingQnA summary of the SEBI report, 21 Aug 2026](https://tradingqna.com/t/win-rate-capital-experience-what-sebi-found-about-f-o-traders/197266/1)
- Win/loss asymmetry: 84.6% of ~4.02 crore trader-quarters were loss-making. Among traders with both winning and losing quarters, 78.7% had a larger average loss in losing quarters than average gain in winning quarters. — [TradingQnA summary](https://tradingqna.com/t/win-rate-capital-experience-what-sebi-found-about-f-o-traders/197266/1)
- Capital, using peak margin as the proxy:
  - About 77% of traders used under Rs 1 lakh, and ~90% of them lost money.
  - Traders using under Rs 10,000 had an average loss per loser of ~Rs 5,600.
  - In the Rs 10 lakh to Rs 1 crore band, ~67.6% were loss-makers.
  — [TradingQnA summary](https://tradingqna.com/t/win-rate-capital-experience-what-sebi-found-about-f-o-traders/197266/1)
- Experience: the share of loss-makers was ~91% in year 1, 94.4% in year 2, 96% in year 3 and 96.5% in year 4; ~95.3% had a cumulative loss at 5 years. Traders with losses in each of the prior two years who kept trading lost again at ~90-92%. Over FY22-FY26, 0.5% were profitable in all five years and 65.6% lost in every year. The five-year profitable group averaged Rs 66.47 lakh profit. — [TradingQnA summary](https://tradingqna.com/t/win-rate-capital-experience-what-sebi-found-about-f-o-traders/197266/1); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/sebi-futures-options-study-retail-traders-trading-losses-experience)
- Trading frequency: traders active more than 100 days were 42% of traders but 94% of turnover and 87% of losses, with an average loss of ~Rs 2.76 lakh. Traders active 100 days or fewer averaged ~Rs 22,000 loss. — [TradingQnA summary](https://tradingqna.com/t/win-rate-capital-experience-what-sebi-found-about-f-o-traders/197266/1)

**SEBI study, FY25 (July 2025)**
- 91% of individual traders lost money, with net losses of Rs 1,05,603 crore after costs (FY24: Rs 74,812 crore). The sample was the top 13 brokers, covering ~96 lakh unique traders. Unique traders fell from 61.4 lakh in Q1 to 42.7 lakh in Q4 FY25. — [Business Standard, 7 Jul 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/net-losses-of-traders-in-fo-widens-in-fy25-sebi-study-125070701221_1.html); [Value Research](https://www.valueresearchonline.com/stories/225398/average-trader-lost-rs-1-1-lakh-fo-fy25/)
- Data conflict: the FY26 study's comparison figure for FY25 losses (Rs 1,11,788 crore) differs from the July 2025 study's Rs 1,05,603 crore. This is likely a revised sample or method; both numbers are cited above.

**SEBI study, FY22-FY24 (press release PR 22/2024, 23 Sep 2024)**
- 93% of over 1 crore individual traders lost money, averaging ~Rs 2 lakh each including costs, and over Rs 1.8 lakh crore in aggregate.
- Only 7.2% made a profit over the three years, and only ~1% made more than Rs 1 lakh after costs.
- The top 3.5% of loss-makers (~4 lakh traders) lost ~Rs 28 lakh each.
- Over 75% of loss-makers kept trading.
- In FY24, prop traders had gross profits of Rs 33,000 crore and FPIs Rs 28,000 crore. 96% of prop profits and 97% of FPI profits came from algorithmic trading.
— [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/markets/fo-frenzy-over-93-retail-investors-lost-average-rs-2-lakh-in-three-years-says-sebi); [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/93-of-1-crore-fo-traders-saw-rs-2l-avg-loss-while-top-3-5-faced-rs-28l-avg-loss-incl-costs-sebi/)

**SEBI structural measures (FY25):** SEBI made six changes:
- one weekly expiry per exchange;
- higher minimum contract size;
- upfront collection of option premium;
- removal of calendar-spread margin benefit on expiry day;
- extra extreme loss margin (ELM) on short options on expiry day;
- intraday monitoring of position limits.
Options participation fell 25.8% between Q2 and Q4 FY25. SEBI says the study does not establish causality. — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/story/rs91685-cr-lost-88-of-individual-traders-lost-money-in-fy26-options-drove-92-of-losses-550474-2026-08-21)

### Inferences
- The SEBI data describe **who loses less**, not a recipe for profit. Losses are smaller among traders who:
  - trade fewer days (100 days or fewer per year: ~Rs 22k average loss vs Rs 2.76 lakh);
  - use small capital (under Rs 10k margin: ~Rs 5.6k average loss);
  - are older (over 60: 81% loss rate vs 89% under 30);
  - trade futures rather than options (~66% vs ~88% loss rate).
- Option sellers lose less often (~44%) but have huge tail losses. For a Rs 10-25k buyer, selling naked is not an option anyway (margin), and the seller evidence argues for defined-risk structures rather than naked selling.
- Experience does not rescue retail traders on average. A "keep going until skilled" plan is not supported by the evidence. Hard stop criteria for the whole experiment are warranted.
- Costs are a first-order problem. With costs equal to 35% of losers' gross losses, a strategy whose gross edge is under ~1-2 rupees of spread-plus-charges per lot round trip is likely negative.
- Profit at scale accrues to algorithmic institutions. A retail buyer is mostly the counterparty to them, especially in 0DTE contracts.

### Gaps
- I did not read the SEBI FY25-FY26 primary PDF. Buyer/seller, capital and experience figures come from a Zerodha forum summary and press coverage.
- I found no SEBI breakdown of the *characteristics of the profitable minority* by holding period, intraday vs positional, or strategy type. The studies mostly profile losers.
- I found no SEBI data specifically for small option buyers with 1-2 day holds.

## 2. International evidence: how many persist in profitability and what predicts it

### Takeaway
Taiwan: under 1-3% of day traders are predictably profitable. Brazil: 97% of persistent futures day traders lost money. US: retail options buyers lost over $1 billion before costs plus ~$4.1 billion in spread costs. The only robust predictor of future profit is a long, statistically meaningful record of past profit. Most traders never get one, and most losers keep trading anyway.

### Cited Findings
- **Taiwan (Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean, Zhang, "Do Day Traders Rationally Learn About Their Ability?", Oct 2017 draft; complete TWSE data 1992-2006):**
  - Aggregate day-trader performance net of fees was negative in every one of the 15 years.
  - More than 75% of day traders quit within two years, and poor performers are more likely to quit.
  - Previously unprofitable traders with 50 or more days of experience still had a 95.3% probability of day trading again in the next 12 months, vs 96.4% for profitable ones.
  - "An extensive history of profitability is a strong predictor of future profit." Profitable traders with more than 40 days of day trading in the past year earned enough to cover costs.
  - Predictably profitable traders are "less than 3% of all day traders on an average day" and 9.81% of volume. An earlier paper (Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean 2010) put them at "less than 1% of the day trading population".
  - Nearly 3/4 of day trading is by traders with a history of losses. In the authors' words, "trading to learn" is no more rational than "playing roulette to learn."
  — [Barber et al. 2017 PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/day%20trading%20and%20learning%20110217.pdf)
- **Taiwan, earlier paper (2004):** over 80% of day traders lose money after costs. The most active earned 31 bps per day gross, which was insufficient to cover costs, while a small persistent group earned 62 bps. The authors cite "practice and speed" as success factors. — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/do-day-traders-make-money/)
- **Brazil (Chague, De-Losso, Giovannetti, "Day Trading for a Living?", FGV WP 525, 2019/2020):** covers equity-futures (mini-index) day traders who started 2013-2015 and persisted more than 300 days.
  - 97% lost money.
  - Only 1.1% (17 of 1,551) earned more than the minimum wage, and 0.5% more than a bank teller's starting salary.
  - The authors found no evidence of learning by day trading.
  — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101); [FGV repository](https://repositorio.fgv.br/items/e87d04fc-4b4c-4a56-ab5a-1c84c1afde1b)
- **US options (Bryzgalova, Pavlova, Sikorskaya, "Retail Trading in Options and the Rise of the Big Three Wholesalers", 2022 WP; Journal of Finance 2023):**
  - Retail traders prefer cheap, weekly options. 50% of wholesaler (retail) trades were in options with less than a week to expiry, with an average quoted bid-ask spread of 12.3% (12.6% in the published version).
  - Slightly-OTM options had a 28% average spread, and "micro" trades of $250 or less had 23.4%.
  - 69% of retail volume was calls; 72% of trades were ATM and 24% slightly OTM.
  - Retail lost $1.14 billion at a 10-day horizon (Nov 2019-Jun 2021) before costs, while all options trades gained $5.48 billion. Losses were concentrated in short-dated (under 1 week) ATM/slightly ITM calls.
  - Effective trading costs (trade price vs midquote) were ~$4.13 billion, far above the ~$800 million in commissions.
  — [Paper PDF (NBER conference version)](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f163907/f163907.pdf); [LBS listing](https://www.london.edu/faculty-and-research/academic-research/r/retail-trading-in-options-and-the-rise-of-the-big-three-wholesalers)

### Inferences
- Across three markets, the persistently profitable share is about 0.5-3%. The most credible predictor is a *long, large-sample* record of net profit, not short streaks. For the assistant, "skill" should be judged only on statistically meaningful samples; see section 4.
- For a retail options buyer, the spread is often a larger cost than the directional loss. US evidence shows effective spread costs about 4x the directional loss. Spread control is the single most actionable safeguard.
- Losers persist at nearly the same rate as winners. A pre-committed, automated kill-switch counters this documented bias.

### Gaps
- I did not find the Bryzgalova et al. numbers on per-trade percentage losses for buyers vs sellers, or the published JF tables.
- I did not find studies of persistence specific to *options buyers* (as opposed to stock or futures day traders) outside the SEBI data.

## 3. Risk-management practices with evidence

### Takeaway
Hard academic evidence exists for:
- avoiding wide-spread, short-dated and OTM contracts (cost evidence);
- trading less often (SEBI frequency data);
- conditional value of stop-losses (they help only when returns trend);
- the seller's structural edge, the volatility risk premium (VRP).
Fixed-fraction sizing, daily-loss caps, trade-count limits and cooldowns are mostly **[practitioner]** rules. They are justified by the behavioural evidence (overtrading, persistence after losses) rather than by direct RCT-style studies.

### Cited Findings
- **Stop-losses (Kaminski & Lo, "When Do Stop-Loss Rules Stop Losses?", J. Financial Markets 2014):**
  - Under a random walk, simple stop-loss rules always reduce expected return.
  - They add value only when returns show momentum (serial correlation).
  - A working-paper version found stop-loss rules added 50-100 bps per month during stop-out periods (monthly data 1950-2004).
  — [MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/114876)
- **Volatility risk premium (VRP) in India:**
  - A Journal of Futures Markets paper on Indian option prices reports significant daily returns to VRP-harvesting (option-selling) strategies, "substantially reduced" after normal transaction costs. — [MetaTOC listing](https://metatoc.com/papers/41285-volatility-risk-premium-in-indian-options-prices)
  - Mishra & Panda (2016) find Nifty implied volatilities are biased estimates of realized volatility. — [University of Hyderabad repository](https://dspace.uohyd.ac.in/handle/1/4779)
  - Evidence is mixed across Indian studies.
- **Cost evidence for contract selection:** short-dated, OTM and tiny-ticket options carry the widest spreads: 12.3% (under 1 week), 28% (slightly OTM) and 23.4% (trades of $250 or less). — [Bryzgalova et al.](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f163907/f163907.pdf)
- **Frequency:** Indian traders active more than 100 days per year account for 87% of losses (avg ~Rs 2.76 lakh) vs ~Rs 22k for traders active 100 days or fewer. — [TradingQnA SEBI summary](https://tradingqna.com/t/win-rate-capital-experience-what-sebi-found-about-f-o-traders/197266/1)
- **Seller vs buyer:** SEBI FY26 sample: ~44% of majorly-option-sellers lost money vs ~90% of only-buyers, but sellers' average loss among losers was ~Rs 51.7 lakh. — [TradingQnA SEBI summary](https://tradingqna.com/t/win-rate-capital-experience-what-sebi-found-about-f-o-traders/197266/1)
- **[practitioner] Fixed-fractional sizing:**
  - Indian F&O guides cite risking 0.5-2% of account capital per trade. — [Sahi guide](https://www.sahi.com/blogs/position-sizing-fno-trading-india-guide); [Groww blog](https://groww.in/blog/position-sizing)
  - For long options, assume a 50-100% premium loss when sizing; one guide shows that a 2% budget may not afford even one lot. — [TradingView practitioner post](https://in.tradingview.com/chart/TRADEWELL/4oLea4uv-Risk-Management-Position-Sizing/)
  - Zerodha Varsity has a position-sizing module, but I did not verify its exact thresholds. — [TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/how-to-do-risk-management-and-position-sizing-while-trading-nifty-bank-nifty/105049)

### Inferences
- **Stop-loss implication:** for an intraday-to-2-day buyer, a stop only makes sense if the entry signal is momentum-based. Mean-reversion entries plus tight stops are the worst combination per Kaminski & Lo. Long options already have defined max loss (the premium), so a stop's role is to cap loss below 100% of premium and to free capital.
- **Defined-risk debit spreads vs naked buys:** a debit spread sells a second leg that partly recovers the VRP. That cuts net premium, theta and vega exposure, but it doubles the spread cost and the number of charge legs. With Indian charges (STT, brokerage per order) this matters at Rs 10-25k ticket size. It needs testing with real costs rather than assuming it is better. **[inference; no direct Indian study found]**
- **Sizing at this account size:** at Rs 10-25k per trade, a 2% risk rule implies an account of Rs 5-12.5 lakh if the full premium is at risk. Otherwise the per-trade risk is far above "2%." The assistant should compute risk as a percentage of the dedicated trading capital and flag when one lot exceeds the cap.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed study testing maximum-daily-loss rules, cooldowns or trade-count caps on retail options outcomes. Their support is behavioural or practitioner.
- I found no Indian study comparing debit spreads vs naked long options net of costs for retail.

## 4. How to evaluate a strategy honestly

### Takeaway
Data snooping is the main reason strategies that work on paper fail live. Require out-of-sample confirmation, adjust significance for the number of variants tried (t-stat above ~3 rather than 2), include full costs and realistic fills, and treat paper results as an upper bound.

### Cited Findings
- Harvey, Liu & Zhu ("...and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns", RFS 29(1) 2016) say that, given multiple testing, a new factor needs a t-ratio above 3.0, not 2.0. — [NBER w20592](https://nber.org/papers/w20592)
- Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu ("The Probability of Backtest Overfitting", J. Computational Finance 20(4), 2017):
  - They propose combinatorially symmetric cross-validation (CSCV) to estimate the probability of backtest overfitting.
  - They argue simple hold-out tests are unreliable for backtests.
  - The more configurations tried, the higher the overfitting probability, so the minimum backtest length (MinBTL) must grow with the number of trials.
  - The Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR) adjusts for the number of trials and non-normal returns.
  — [SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253); [eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/content/qt4w1110bb/qt4w1110bb.pdf)
- Costs dominate for retail options: effective spread costs were ~$4.13 billion vs ~$0.8 billion in commissions. — [Bryzgalova et al.](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f163907/f163907.pdf) Indian costs equal 35% of losers' gross losses. — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/story/rs91685-cr-lost-88-of-individual-traders-lost-money-in-fy26-options-drove-92-of-losses-550474-2026-08-21)
- Taiwan evidence: past profitability predicts future profit only for long histories (40+ days of day trading in the past year, among profitable traders). — [Barber et al. 2017](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/day%20trading%20and%20learning%20110217.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a paper-trading phase, suggested criteria before going live **[inference/practitioner]**:
  - (a) Pre-register the rules and freeze parameters.
  - (b) Run at least 100 closed paper trades, or at least 3 months, whichever is longer. Per-trade P&L on long options is highly skewed, so 30 trades is too few.
  - (c) Require a t-stat of mean net P&L per trade of at least 2, and preferably 3 if many variants were tried.
  - (d) Price fills at the ask for buys and the bid for sells (not LTP or mid), plus all statutory charges.
  - (e) Haircut paper P&L by an extra slippage factor before comparing with the go-live threshold.
- Track live-vs-paper slippage per trade once real trading starts. Pause if realized slippage exceeds the modelled amount.

### Gaps
- I found no published quantitative measure of the paper-vs-live slippage gap for Indian retail options.
- I did not extract exact MinBTL formula numbers. The rule of thumb is that MinBTL grows with log(number of trials).

## 5. Specific measurable safeguards and thresholds

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence-backed thresholds concern **contract selection**:
- avoid 0DTE and expiry-day trades;
- avoid wide spreads;
- avoid deep OTM and very cheap premiums;
- prefer liquid index options.
They also concern **activity limits**: fewer trading days, and a kill-switch after sustained losses. Exact numeric cut-offs are mostly practitioner or inferred and should be treated as tunable defaults.

### Cited Findings
- **Expiry concentration in India:** 59% of FY26 index-option turnover was on expiry day and 97% within a week. SEBI added ELM on short options and removed calendar-spread benefits on expiry day, signalling expiry-day risk. — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/story/rs91685-cr-lost-88-of-individual-traders-lost-money-in-fy26-options-drove-92-of-losses-550474-2026-08-21)
- **Spread levels:** short-dated retail option spreads average 12.3%, slightly-OTM 28%, micro trades 23.4% (US). — [Bryzgalova et al.](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f163907/f163907.pdf)
- **Trade frequency:** more active traders account for 87% of losses. — [TradingQnA SEBI summary](https://tradingqna.com/t/win-rate-capital-experience-what-sebi-found-about-f-o-traders/197266/1)
- **Persistence after losses:** about 90-92% of two-year losers lose again in year three, and over 75% of losers keep trading. — [TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/win-rate-capital-experience-what-sebi-found-about-f-o-traders/197266/1); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/markets/fo-frenzy-over-93-retail-investors-lost-average-rs-2-lakh-in-three-years-says-sebi)
- **Win/loss asymmetry:** 78.7% of mixed-outcome traders had larger average losses than gains. — [TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/win-rate-capital-experience-what-sebi-found-about-f-o-traders/197266/1)
- **[practitioner] Risk per trade:** 0.5-2% of capital. — [Sahi](https://www.sahi.com/blogs/position-sizing-fno-trading-india-guide)

### Inferences: proposed enforceable safeguards (defaults to tune in paper trading)
| Safeguard | Proposed default | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| No 0DTE / expiry-day entries | Block entries in a contract on its expiry day; prefer at least 2-3 trading days to expiry at entry, and close at least 1 day before expiry for 2-day holds | SEBI expiry concentration; US losses concentrated in under-1-week options (evidence); exact days are inference |
| Max bid-ask spread | Block if (ask-bid)/mid is above 2-3% for index options or above 5% for stock options; size cost into expected edge | Spread is the dominant retail cost (evidence); thresholds are inference |
| Liquidity floor | Index options or F&O stocks only; minimum OI/volume rank near ATM (e.g. within ±3 strikes of ATM) | Wide spreads in OTM/illiquid contracts (evidence); strike band is practitioner |
| Minimum premium | Avoid premiums so low that the tick and charges exceed ~2% of premium (e.g. Nifty under ~Rs 20) | Micro/cheap trades carry 23-28% spreads in US data; threshold is inference |
| Moneyness | ATM to 1 strike OTM; no deep OTM "lottery" buys | Spread and VRP evidence |
| IV filter | Avoid buying when IV percentile is high (e.g. above 70-80th percentile of 1y) unless an event is the thesis; consider debit spreads instead | VRP favours sellers, especially in high-IV regimes (mixed Indian evidence); threshold is practitioner |
| Risk per trade | Max loss per trade at most 2% of dedicated capital (premium x stop%); flag if one lot exceeds the cap | Practitioner |
| Max trades per day | 1-2 new entries per day; at most ~3-5 per week | SEBI frequency/loss link (evidence); numbers practitioner |
| Daily loss cap / cooldown | Stop for the day after a 2-3% capital drawdown or 2 consecutive losses; 1-day cooldown | Persistence-after-loss bias (evidence); numbers practitioner |
| Kill-switch | Halt to paper-only if 20-trade rolling net P&L is negative and the t-stat is below 0, or if drawdown exceeds 10-15% of capital; full review required to restart | Losers persist and experience does not help (evidence); numbers inference |
| Go-live gate | At least 100 paper trades, positive net expectancy after ask/bid fills and full charges, t-stat of at least 2 (3 if many variants), out-of-sample period not used for tuning | Harvey-Liu-Zhu; Bailey et al. |
| Costs model | Every P&L includes brokerage, STT, exchange fees, GST, stamp duty and SEBI fee, plus spread crossing | Costs equal 35% of losers' gross losses (evidence) |

### Gaps
- I found no credible academic source giving exact numeric thresholds for days-to-expiry, IV percentile, maximum spread, minimum premium or cooldown length for Indian retail buyers. All numbers in the table are inference or practitioner defaults to be calibrated in paper trading.
- I did not verify Zerodha Varsity's or NSE's own investor-education thresholds.
