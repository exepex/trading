# Retail option buyers fund India's algo desks

Nine in ten Indian retail F&O traders lose money in every year SEBI has measured, and option buyers are the core of the problem. Options produce about 92% of individual losses, and about 90% of pure option buyers lose. In FY26, the first full year after SEBI's 2024 curbs, 87.7% of individuals still lost a combined Rs 91,685 crore. The money flows mostly to proprietary trading firms and foreign portfolio investors (FPIs), and 96-99% of their profits come from algorithmic trading. Buyers lose for structural reasons before direction even matters. Options lose value every hour (time decay). They are often bought when they are expensive, before events, and then cheapen afterwards (volatility crush). Cheap far-from-the-money strikes rarely pay off. Spreads and charges take a fixed slice of every trade. Most retail turnover sits in contracts expiring the same day, where all of these effects are at their worst. The profitable minority is tiny: 0.5% of traders were profitable in all five years from FY22 to FY26. Experience does not help, because loss rates *rise* with years of trading. So the tool cannot rely on finding an edge. It has to enforce rules that keep losses small and rare: no expiry-day buying, strict spread and premium floors, few trades, risk capped as a share of capital, an automatic kill-switch, and a paper-trading go-live gate that requires statistically meaningful profits after realistic costs. A prioritised safeguards table is at the end. Throughout, **Evidence** marks what a source states, and **Inference** marks my own reasoning, arithmetic or design choices.

A note on sources: the SEBI study PDFs could not be read directly. SEBI figures come from press and broker summaries that quote them, and the sources sometimes differ slightly. Where they do, both figures are given.

## Ten terms the rest of this report depends on

A non-specialist needs a small vocabulary to follow the mechanics. Each term below is used later in the report.

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| Option, premium | The right (not the obligation) to buy (call) or sell (put) an index or stock at a fixed price (the *strike*) until expiry. The buyer pays a *premium*, and that premium is the most the buyer can lose. |
| Lot | The fixed contract size. Nifty is 65 units and Bank Nifty is 30, from January 2026. Cost of one lot = premium × lot size. |
| Expiry, DTE, 0DTE | The date the contract ends. DTE means days to expiry. 0DTE means buying an option on the day it expires. Nifty weeklies expire on Tuesday, and Bank Nifty is now monthly only. |
| ATM / OTM / ITM | At-, out-of- or in-the-money: the strike is near, away from (worthless if expiry were now), or through the current price. |
| Theta (time decay) | The value an option loses each day if nothing else changes. It speeds up sharply in the last 1-3 days. |
| Implied volatility (IV), IV crush | The size of future swings that the option's price assumes. It rises before known events and collapses after them ("crush"), which cuts premiums even when the direction was right. |
| Delta, gamma | Delta is roughly how much the option moves per 1-point move in the underlying, and also a rough probability of finishing in the money. Gamma is how fast delta changes. It is extreme near expiry, which makes 0DTE options behave like lottery tickets. |
| Bid-ask spread | The gap between the best price to sell (bid) and the best price to buy (ask). Crossing it is a cost on every round trip. |
| Open interest (OI) | The number of contracts currently open at a strike. Retail sees it end-of-day; large intraday positions are invisible in it. |
| Prop trader, FPI, algo | A proprietary firm trading its own money, a foreign portfolio investor, and computer-driven trading. These are retail's main counterparties. |

## Nine in ten lose every year, and the 2024 curbs shrank the crowd rather than the odds

SEBI has published four rounds of study, and they all point the same way. **January 2023 (FY22 data):** 89% of individual traders lost money, with an average loss of Rs 1.1 lakh. Loss-makers paid a further **28% of their net trading loss in transaction costs**. Even profitable traders gave 15-50% of their profit to costs. Trader numbers at the top 10 brokers rose from 7.1 lakh to 45.2 lakh in three years ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/markets/stocks/story/loss-makers-of-dalal-street-9-out-of-10-individual-fo-traders-lost-money-in-fy22-says-sebi-367628-2023-01-25)). This study is the origin of the "9 out of 10 traders incurred net losses" disclaimer shown at broker login ([NSDL](https://nsdl.co.in/publications/risk_Disclosure.php)).

**September 2024 (FY22-FY24 data):** **93% of more than 1 crore individuals lost money**, averaging about Rs 2 lakh each including costs, more than Rs 1.8 lakh crore in total. **Only about 1% made more than Rs 1 lakh after costs** ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/markets/fo-frenzy-over-93-retail-investors-lost-average-rs-2-lakh-in-three-years-says-sebi)). The FY24 split shows where the money went. Before costs, prop traders made about Rs 33,000 crore and FPIs about Rs 28,000 crore. Individuals lost about Rs 55,000 crore in options but *made* about Rs 13,400 crore in futures ([TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/sebis-latest-analysis-of-profits-losses-in-the-equity-derivatives-segment-fy22-fy24/173596)). The same study found that 76.3% of traders who lost in both FY22 and FY23 kept trading in FY24, and only 8.3% of them made a profit that year ([TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/sebis-latest-analysis-of-profits-losses-in-the-equity-derivatives-segment-fy22-fy24/173596)).

**July 2025 (FY25 data):** FY25 was the worst year. About 91% lost, and net losses rose 41% to Rs 1,05,603 crore, from Rs 74,812 crore in FY24 ([The Week/PTI](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2025/07/07/dcm92-biz-sebi-derivatives-study.html)). The 2026 study later revised the FY25 total to Rs 1,11,788 crore.

**August 2026 (FY25-FY26 data):** these were two studies by SEBI's economic research department. In FY26, active traders fell 18% and aggregate losses fell to **Rs 91,685 crore**, with **87.7% losing**. But the **average loss per remaining trader rose slightly, to about Rs 1.17 lakh** ([The Policy Edge](https://www.policyedge.in/p/nearly-9-in-10-individual-fo-traders-lost-money-as-participation-fell-18-sebi-studies)). The Rs 1.17 lakh is averaged over all traders; per loss-maker the average was Rs 1.47 lakh ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/story/rs91685-cr-lost-88-of-individual-traders-lost-money-in-fy26-options-drove-92-of-losses-550474-2026-08-21)). Options drove 92% of losses. Individuals paid Rs 24,859 crore in transaction costs, and **about 4.4 lakh traders who were profitable before costs ended up losing after costs** ([The Policy Edge](https://www.policyedge.in/p/nearly-9-in-10-individual-fo-traders-lost-money-as-participation-fell-18-sebi-studies)). Costs equalled **35% of loss-makers' gross losses** and 21% of profit-makers' gross profits. Brokerage was 44% of costs, STT (the securities transaction tax) 27%, exchange charges 16% and GST 11% ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/story/rs91685-cr-lost-88-of-individual-traders-lost-money-in-fy26-options-drove-92-of-losses-550474-2026-08-21)).

| Study (data years) | Share of individuals losing | Headline loss | Key extra finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jan 2023 (FY22) | 89% | Avg Rs 1.1 lakh | Costs added 28% on top of net losses |
| Sep 2024 (FY22-24) | 93% | Rs 1.8 lakh crore total | Only ~1% made >Rs 1 lakh; prop + FPI made ~Rs 61,000 crore gross in FY24 |
| Jul 2025 (FY25) | ~91% | Rs 1,05,603 crore (later revised to Rs 1,11,788 crore) | Traders down 20%, losses up 41% |
| Aug 2026 (FY26) | 87.7% | Rs 91,685 crore | Options = 92% of losses; avg loss per trader up 2.4% |

**Inference:** the curbs reduced how many people traded and the total amount lost. They did not make the remaining traders meaningfully less likely to lose, or lose less each. A tool built in 2026 cannot assume regulation has made retail option buying a fair game. Trader counts also differ between sources, for example 87.5 lakh vs 78.6 lakh for FY26 ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/markets/sebi-measures-reduce-equity-fo-losses-for-retail-investors-in-fy26)). The difference probably reflects definitions ("active traders" vs "unique investors"), but no source confirms that.

## Option buyers lose to time, volatility and costs before direction even matters

The FY26 behavioural sample of 5,050 traders shows how one-sided retail is. **About 93% only buy options**, and **about 90% of them lose money**. By contrast, about 44% of the ~2% who mainly sell options lose ([TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/win-rate-capital-experience-what-sebi-found-about-f-o-traders/197266)). A buyer can be right about direction and still lose, for five overlapping reasons.

### Time decay concentrates in the last two days

An option's time value shrinks faster as expiry approaches. Zerodha Varsity's worked example shows time value falling from Rs 5-8 with 20 or more days left to about 30 paise with one day left ([Zerodha Varsity](https://zerodha.com/varsity/chapter/theta/)). Retail trades exactly where decay is steepest. **59% of FY26 index-option turnover was in same-day (0DTE) contracts, 75% within one day of expiry, and 97% within a week.** Contracts with more than 7 days left were only 3% of turnover ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/story/rs91685-cr-lost-88-of-individual-traders-lost-money-in-fy26-options-drove-92-of-losses-550474-2026-08-21)).

**Inference (my Black-Scholes approximation, not a sourced figure):** take Nifty at 25,000 with implied volatility of 12% and an unchanged spot. An at-the-money option is worth about Rs 166 with 7 days left, Rs 89 with 2 days and Rs 63 with 1 day. Holding from 2 DTE to 1 DTE costs about 30% of the premium overnight. On expiry morning the same option is worth about Rs 31 and goes to about zero by 15:30 if spot does not move, so the payoff is close to binary.

### Volatility crush punishes correct calls around events

Implied volatility is bid up before scheduled events and collapses after them. India VIX, the market's implied-volatility index, fell about 34% and 30% on the 2014 and 2019 election results days ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/india-vix-index-sharply-slumps-20-the-biggest-drop-in-five-years-124042301001_1.html)). It fell 12-17% on Budget day in February 2025 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/capital-market-news/indices-trade-with-tiny-losses-vix-drops-over-12-125020100994_1.html)). Surprises can do the opposite. VIX spiked on the 2024 election results day, then fell 25% the next day ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/capital-market-news/nifty-above-21-950-mark-fmcg-shares-advance-vix-slumps-25-32-124060500281_1.html)). It rose about 11% on Budget day 2026, when the Budget raised F&O STT ([HSBC MF](https://www.assetmanagement.hsbc.co.in/assets/documents/mutual-funds/en/0b268914-8e60-438e-810f-5764bf6bb00e/monday-market-flash-february-02-2026.pdf)).

**Inference:** an ATM option's premium moves roughly in proportion to IV. A 25% IV drop therefore removes about 25% of the premium with no price move at all. That is enough to wipe out a correct directional call held through results, an RBI policy decision or a Budget.

### Cheap far-OTM options are lottery tickets with worse odds

Research on NSE found that when lot sizes were raised, trading shifted to *cheaper, riskier* out-of-the-money options. The authors read this as small accounts plus a preference for lottery-like payoffs ([Agarwal & Ghosh, CFR WP 25-09](https://www.cfr-cologne.de/download/workingpaper/cfr-25-09.pdf)). **Inference (my calculation):** with Nifty at 25,000, IV at 12% and one day left, a strike 300 points out of the money has about a 3% chance of finishing in the money, and a strike 500 points away has well under 0.1%.

### Spreads and fixed charges take a bigger slice of cheaper options

US data on retail options are the clearest available evidence. Retail options lost about **$1.14 billion before costs**, but paid about **$4.13 billion in effective spread costs**, against about $0.8 billion in commissions. Quoted spreads averaged **12.3% for options with less than a week to expiry, 28% for slightly-OTM options and 23.4% for trades of $250 or less** ([Bryzgalova, Pavlova & Sikorskaya](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f163907/f163907.pdf)). In India, what matters is the spread *relative to the premium*: a Rs 2 spread is 1% of a Rs 200 premium but 10% of a Rs 20 premium ([AlgoTest](https://algotest.in/blog/what-is-liquidity-in-trading/)).

From 1 April 2026, STT on options is 0.15% of premium on the sell side, and 0.15% of intrinsic value (the amount an option is in the money at expiry) on exercise ([Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/stt-hike-from-april-1-2026-budget-what-it-means-for-futures-and-options-traders)). **Inference:** brokerage is charged per order (a flat Rs 20 is common, but I did not verify Groww's current schedule). For a Rs 10 premium, that fixed charge plus a one-tick spread means the option has to move about 7-10% in your favour just to break even. For a Rs 100 premium the same costs are under about 1%.

### Overtrading and behaviour compound everything

The behaviour data show the same pattern from several angles. **Traders active more than 100 days a year were 42% of traders but produced 94% of turnover and 87% of losses**, averaging about Rs 2.76 lakh lost, against about Rs 22,000 for those active 100 days or fewer ([TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/win-rate-capital-experience-what-sebi-found-about-f-o-traders/197266/1)). **78.7% of traders with both winning and losing quarters had bigger average losses than gains** ([TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/win-rate-capital-experience-what-sebi-found-about-f-o-traders/197266/1)). This is the footprint of the disposition effect: taking profits early and letting losses run. An ISB study found Indian retail equity investors show this effect more strongly than investors in the US, Japan, China or Finland ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/markets/retail-investors-tend-to-lose-in-stock-markets-says-isb-study-112120100043_1.html)). In SEBI's intraday cash-equity study, the loss rate rose from 71% overall to 80% for traders making more than 500 trades a year ([Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/7-out-of-10-individual-intraday-traders-in-equity-cash-segment-make-losses-sebi-study/74733.html)).

## Algorithmic desks collect what retail loses, legally and allegedly otherwise

**Evidence:** in FY26, prop traders made about Rs 44,000 crore and FPIs about Rs 14,000 crore gross, and **99% of their profits went to entities using algorithms** ([Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/92-percentage-of-aggregate-losses-incurred-by-individuals-are-from-options-trading-sebi-study/81429.html)). Algorithms account for about 70% of F&O trading ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/amp/story/markets/algos-have-rewritten-the-script-for-trading-in-india-its-time-for-retail-investors-to-play-it-smart)). Most of this profit is structural rather than illegal. Option sellers collect the time value and the "volatility risk premium" (options are on average priced for bigger moves than actually happen) that buyers pay. An Indian study finds significant returns to selling volatility, though much smaller after transaction costs ([MetaTOC listing](https://metatoc.com/papers/41285-volatility-risk-premium-in-indian-options-prices)).

Selling is not free money for retail either. Losing retail sellers lost **about Rs 51.7 lakh on average** in FY26, against about Rs 1.3 lakh for losing buyers ([TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/win-rate-capital-experience-what-sebi-found-about-f-o-traders/197266)). That is the "tail risk" of selling: frequent small wins and rare huge losses. Desks with scale, hedging and low costs capture the edge. A Rs 10k-25k account cannot.

The **Jane Street case** shows the manipulation channel. Everything below is **allegation from an ex-parte interim order (issued without hearing the firm), not an adjudicated finding.** SEBI's 3 July 2025 order alleges the following on 17 January 2024, a Bank Nifty expiry day. From 09:15 to about 11:47, Jane Street entities bought Rs 4,370 crore of Bank Nifty stocks and futures, lifting the index from 46,574 to 47,177, while building bearish options positions. From 11:49 they sold, pushing the index down in line with the options book, and made Rs 734.93 crore that day ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/commoditiesderivativesstock-exchanges/1648134/sebis-interim-order-against-jane-street-allegations-of-index-manipulation-explained)). On other days SEBI alleges "extended marking the close": large one-way trades in the last two hours of expiry days ([Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/jane-street-accused-of-massively-rigging-of-indias-derivatives-market-sebi-orders-ban-escrow-of-rs4843-crore-illegal-gains/77585.html)). SEBI put the group's index-options profit for January 2023 to March 2025 at Rs 44,358 crore, and said the activity "misled lakhs of retail options traders" ([Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/jane-street-accused-of-massively-rigging-of-indias-derivatives-market-sebi-orders-ban-escrow-of-rs4843-crore-illegal-gains/77585.html)).

Jane Street deposited Rs 4,843.57 crore in escrow and its trading restrictions were lifted in July 2025 ([ECGI](https://www.ecgi.global/publications/blog/expiry-day-and-the-governance-of-algorithmic-trading-the-jane-street-episode)). As of 5 October 2026 the matter is still before the Securities Appellate Tribunal (SAT) and there is no final order. Jane Street argues that NSE's and SEBI's own initial surveillance reviews found no manipulation ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328567/20261005/jane-street-tells-india-tribunal-sebis-own-reports-found-no-manipulation-before-567m-order.htm)). SEBI later capped intraday index-option positions at Rs 5,000 crore net per entity ([Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/equity/sebi-outlines-new-framework-for-monitoring-of-intraday-position-limits-for-index-derivatives)).

**Inference:** whatever the legal outcome, the pattern is a concrete reason to distrust a strong one-way morning move on expiry day. A move carried by one large participant can reverse in the afternoon.

The closing window is a second, newer hazard. Since **3 August 2026**, more than 200 F&O stocks set their closing price in a Closing Auction Session (CAS) from 15:15. In the first sessions, Nifty's official close was **0.6-0.8% away from its 15:15 level** ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/stocks/story/explained-why-closing-auction-session-is-triggering-sharp-market-swings-547396-2026-08-05)). SEBI's 12 September 2026 consultation paper flagged concentrated trading in expiring options during the auction and proposed changing how expiry settlement prices are set ([Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/amp/story/invest/sebi-proposes-changes-to-closing-auction-session-fo-settlement-rules-key-proposals-explained)). No final decision had been found when these notes were written.

Several popular retail ideas have weak evidence behind them. Strike "pinning" at expiry is documented for US single stocks ([Ni, Pearson & Poteshman, JFE 2005](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X05000577)), but not convincingly for Indian indices. Max pain (the strike where option buyers collectively lose most) is a practitioner heuristic ([TrueData](https://www.truedata.in/blog/max-pain-theory-does-it-really-work)). No Indian study shows that "OI traps" or stop-loss hunting are deliberate tactics. End-of-day OI can, however, miss intraday positions far larger than what it shows, which is why SEBI moved to delta-based "futures-equivalent" OI in 2025 ([Probe42](https://resources.probe42.in/regulatory-updates/sebi-updates/analysis-of-profits-losses-in-the-equity-derivatives-segment/)). **Inference:** the tool should use OI and max pain as context at most, never as entry triggers.

## The profitable minority is tiny, and experience does not grow it

SEBI's data mostly profile losers, not winners. What they show about persistence is stark. **About 91% of first-year traders lose, rising to 96.5% by year four.** **0.5% of FY22-FY26 traders were profitable in all five years, while 65.6% lost in every one.** Traders who lost two years running lost again 90-92% of the time ([TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/win-rate-capital-experience-what-sebi-found-about-f-o-traders/197266/1); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/sebi-futures-options-study-retail-traders-trading-losses-experience)). About 88% of traders with cumulative losses above Rs 10 lakh kept trading ([The Policy Edge](https://www.policyedge.in/p/nearly-9-in-10-individual-fo-traders-lost-money-as-participation-fell-18-sebi-studies)).

International evidence agrees. In Taiwan, predictably profitable day traders were **under 3% of day traders on an average day**, and unprofitable traders kept trading almost as often as profitable ones (95.3% vs 96.4%). The authors concluded that "trading to learn" is no more rational than "playing roulette to learn" ([Barber et al. 2017](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/day%20trading%20and%20learning%20110217.pdf)). In Brazil, **97% of futures day traders who persisted more than 300 days lost money**, and only 1.1% earned more than the minimum wage ([Chague et al., SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101)).

What the data do identify is **who loses less**, which is different from who profits. Losses are smaller for traders who:

- trade 100 days or fewer a year (about Rs 22,000 average loss vs Rs 2.76 lakh);
- use under Rs 10,000 of margin (about Rs 5,600 average loss per loser);
- are over 60 (81% loss rate vs 89% for under-30s);
- trade futures rather than options (about 66% vs 88% loss rate).

Sources: [TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/win-rate-capital-experience-what-sebi-found-about-f-o-traders/197266/1); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/story/rs91685-cr-lost-88-of-individual-traders-lost-money-in-fy26-options-drove-92-of-losses-550474-2026-08-21). The Taiwanese evidence adds one credible marker of genuine skill: **a long, statistically large record of net profit**, not a short winning streak ([Barber et al. 2017](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/day%20trading%20and%20learning%20110217.pdf)).

Because of data snooping (trying many strategy variants until one looks good by chance), that record must be held to a high bar. A finding should clear a t-statistic of about 3 rather than 2 once many variants have been tried ([Harvey, Liu & Zhu, NBER](https://nber.org/papers/w20592)). The more configurations are tried, the higher the probability that a backtest is overfitted ([Bailey et al., SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253)). Stop-losses help only when price moves trend (momentum); in random markets they reduce expected return ([Kaminski & Lo, MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/114876)).

**Inference:** the profitable minority is defined less by clever signals than by surviving long enough, with few enough trades and low enough costs, for a real edge to show up statistically. The tool's job is to make "not proven yet" the default state and to stop the experiment automatically when the evidence turns against it.

## Recent SEBI rules reshape the game for a Rs 10k-25k buyer

The 1 October 2024 circular ([SEBI](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/oct-2024/measures-to-strengthen-equity-index-derivatives-framework-for-increased-investor-protection-and-market-stability_87208.html)) introduced six measures:

- one weekly expiry per exchange, from 20 November 2024, so Bank Nifty, FinNifty and the other NSE weeklies ended;
- a minimum contract value raised to Rs 15-20 lakh;
- an extra 2% margin on short options on expiry day;
- upfront collection of option premium from buyers, from 1 February 2025;
- no calendar-spread margin benefit on expiry day;
- intraday monitoring of position limits ([Zerodha Z-Connect](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/business-updates/sebis-new-rules-for-index-derivatives-heres-whats-changing)).

Later changes followed in 2025 and 2026:

- NSE expiries moved to **Tuesday** from 1 September 2025, and BSE's Sensex to Thursday ([All India Radio](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/nse-bse-swap-derivatives-expiry-days)).
- Lots were cut to **Nifty 65 and Bank Nifty 30** from January 2026 ([Zerodha bulletin](https://zerodha.com/marketintel/bulletin/429705/revision-in-lot-size-of-index-derivative-contracts-from-december-30-2025)).
- A new market-wide position limit (MWPL), the measure that triggers stock F&O bans, took effect on 1 October 2025 ([NSE Clearing via broker](https://www.ricago.com/assets/front/base/file/file_management/3776.pdf)).
- STT on options premium rose from 0.1% to **0.15%** from 1 April 2026 ([5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/news/stt-hike-on-fo-to-take-effect-from-april-1-amid-rising-options-activity)).

SEBI has also regulated finfluencers since August 2024 and has taken enforcement action, including impounding Rs 546 crore from Avadhut Sathe Trading Academy in December 2025 ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/corporate-law/sebi-cracks-down-on-finfluencer-avadhut-sathe-impounds-546-crore-for-providing-unregistered-investment-advice-312335)).

**Inference:** position limits bind firms, not a Rs 25k trader. What binds the tool is the following:

- **Lot sizes change about twice a year**, so lot cost must be read from exchange contract specifications, not hard-coded. For example, a Rs 150 Nifty premium × 65 = Rs 9,750 a lot, so Rs 10k-25k buys one or two lots.
- **Bank Nifty is monthly only**, so it decays more slowly but costs more per lot.
- **Stocks in the MWPL ban list cannot take new positions.**
- **In-the-money stock options are physically settled**, meaning shares must be delivered or paid for. Positions should be closed before expiry.

The parameters of the experiment also carry a hidden sizing problem. The common practitioner rule is to risk 0.5-2% of capital per trade ([Sahi](https://www.sahi.com/blogs/position-sizing-fno-trading-india-guide); [Groww](https://groww.in/blog/position-sizing)). At a Rs 25,000 ticket with a 40% stop, the maximum loss is Rs 10,000, so the 2% rule implies about Rs 5 lakh of dedicated capital. If the whole premium is treated as at risk, as it should be for overnight holds where gaps jump past stops, it implies Rs 12.5 lakh. Five open Rs 25k trades also put Rs 1.25 lakh at stake at once. If they are all Nifty calls, that is effectively one bet, not five.

The repo already has four guards in `fno/live/signals/guards.py`: an F&O ban-list guard, an earnings guard (minimum 2 days before results), a market-stress guard, and an IV-to-realised-volatility cap of 1.6. The table below keeps these and adds or tightens the rest. Each new rule fits naturally as a separate `GuardRule` class.

## Prioritised safeguards the tool should enforce

Evidence strength is graded as follows. **Strong** means direct Indian regulator data or peer-reviewed work supports the rule's direction. **Moderate** means the evidence is relevant but indirect, or comes from another market. **Weak** means it rests on a single case or a heuristic. **Inference** means my arithmetic or design. In almost every row the *direction* of the rule is better supported than the exact *threshold*. Thresholds are defaults to calibrate during paper trading.

They also do not depend on any signal being good. Rows 6-12 shape which contracts the tool buys and how big. Rows 13-18 are cheaper protections with thinner evidence.

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing of the project. The question is less "how do I find winning trades" than "how do I avoid paying the structural costs that sink 90% of buyers": time decay, volatility crush, spreads, fees and turnover. Those costs are mostly paid to algorithmic sellers and market makers whose edge comes from scale, not cleverness. Every SEBI lever since 2024 has thinned the crowd without fixing the odds per trader. For a buyer the binding enemies are therefore within the tool's control: which contracts it buys, how often, when, at what spread, and how firmly it stops itself.

Two points are easy to miss. First, the go-live gate and kill-switch matter more than any signal, because the best evidence on retail traders is that losers keep trading and experience makes the odds worse, not better. An automated, pre-committed exit is the one behaviour the 0.5% persistent winners and the 65.6% persistent losers clearly differ on. Second, the stated parameters (Rs 10k-25k per trade, five open trades) only fit the 2%-risk logic with roughly Rs 5-12.5 lakh of dedicated capital. If the real pot is smaller, the honest move is to shrink the ticket size or the number of trades, not to relax the rule. What remains uncertain is the exact thresholds. No Indian study gives the "right" spread cap, minimum DTE or cooldown, so the paper-trading phase should be treated as calibration, with each threshold logged and tested, not assumed.
