## Q1. Are Review 116's two fixes implemented exactly? — ACCEPT

**(a) Claim meaning — ACCEPT (strong evidence: BR1a items `primary_baseline_rule`, `claim_meaning`, `confirmatory_family`).** One primary baseline is chosen by lowest mean Brier on the declared 2019–2025 exploratory history, with a deterministic tie-break; only T1/EOD against that baseline is confirmatory. Each of the other two receives a descriptive paired difference and 95% interval, never a “beats all” claim. This resolves Review 116's concern. **Real case:** the earlier sizing gave `cal60` versus `trail60` t=+1.65 on all names but −1.64 in Judge G's adjusted window and +3.31 on the top 30. Declaring `cal60` universally superior from one of these comparisons would be unjustified. The new wording does not.

**(b) Study 3 — ACCEPT as a prospective protocol, not a demonstrated error-rate result (strong evidence: the written deterministic procedure; reasoning only: whether it will pass).** BR1a tests both signs **separately**, across both looks, for the whole eligible history and worst year; it jointly selects the per-look rate and its look counts from (0.02, 0.015, 0.01, 0.005). Every candidate must meet per-look false-claim <=0.025 and either-look <=0.045, or no claim is permitted. The selected look 1 then tests false futility at a true +δ improvement, per sign, with threshold <=0.035; thresholds fall to 0.015/0.01/0.005 or futility is removed. No post-freeze tuning is allowed. **Real case:** the parent v4 uncalibrated confirmatory method rejected some nulls far too often; BR1 does not import v4's NIFTY-specific thresholds and instead requires its own measured size check.

## Q2. Can the written procedure still let a false claim or false futility through? — ACCEPT with implementation checks (strong evidence for ordering and definitions; some evidence for extrapolation)

**Study order and fallback.** Study 1 measures agreement between actual spot and the old near-month-future proxy on 128 overlapping sessions. If agreement is below 97%, **all three** baseline choice, regime study and null calibration use only 2024–25, not an invalid 2019–23 proxy. Study 2 measures the worst year, and Study 3 then computes the look dates at each candidate rate before testing it. This is internally consistent. The 2024–25 fallback reduces the range of regimes available for calibration; it cannot justify a stronger out-of-sample guarantee, but that is disclosed and does not justify another design cycle.

**Seed, δ and mirrored futility.** The seed 20261016 is fixed; the judge reruns another seed and checks agreement within 2 Monte Carlo SE. The same **δ = 0.02 × the selected primary's mean Brier from Study 1** determines look sizing and the boundary of false futility. For the mirrored -d orientation, +δ is added to **-d itself**, not to the original d. That is the correct sign: if the original null differences are (−0.01,+0.01), their mean is zero; under either orientation, adding δ gives a true +δ mean. This closes the unit/sign errors from Judge 186.

**One implementation check that matters to an actual verdict (strong statistical reasoning):** the 63-session bootstrap must draw **the same time-block indices for all three daily difference series in each path**, preserving contemporaneous cross-member correlation. “Three pairs' paths drawn together” says this, but the implementation test should explicitly compare aligned indices. Also **centre each null series within the population being tested** (whole history or the separately tested worst year) before generating null paths; otherwise a baseline pair's genuine mean advantage within the worst year is misclassified as a type-I error. Neither needs a new story or a change to the design's decisions: these are precise interpretations to test when writing Study 3. If the study fails, the written fallback is **no confirmatory claim**, not a hand-chosen rate.

**Known limit (reasoning only):** passing simulations resampled from exposed 2019–25 regimes does not establish false-claim control in an unseen regime; two looks and K=3 do not eliminate that risk. No real-money eligibility or F&O profitability follows from a Brier-skill result.
