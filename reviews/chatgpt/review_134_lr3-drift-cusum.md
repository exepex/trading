## 1. Calibrate h from 30–60 independent resampled trades? — PARTIAL

**Some evidence:** fixed h=4 produces an in-control median false alarm at only **40 trades** for the negatively skewed SF sample, versus no alarm in 3,000 BF trades; a common h is therefore inappropriate. Per-version calibration is preferable. But 30–60 close-proxy trades are a small sample of clustered expiry/monthly regimes, and iid resampling can materially understate the chance of consecutive losses. **The floor of 4 does not fix dependence**: SF already has an unacceptable 40-trade median at h=4.

**Small pre-code change:** calibrate with the current iid method as a reproducible reference, but also compute a **chronological circular-block sensitivity with 3 consecutive trades per block** on each registered sample. Freeze the **larger** of the two resulting h values (rounded upward to 0.5), with the existing floor of 4 and ten-years-of-trades target. This is one extra calculation in the same script, not a monitoring service. Report the two values, observed trades, sample span and uncertainty. If a version has too few completed research trades to support even this sensitivity, mark drift monitoring uncalibrated and refuse paper approval under the existing mandatory-field rule. **Reasoning only:** block length 3 is a simple stress check, not a statistically proven optimal length; avoid asserting the calibrated median run length is a true ten-year false-alarm guarantee. **Trade impact:** a too-small h can wrongly pause all books.

## 2. Research mean/SD versus E8 owner-timed paper nets — ACCEPT, with an interpretation requirement

**Strong evidence:** `z=(mu0-net)/sigma` compares actual after-cost paper outcomes with the prospectively registered research expectation. If E8's next-session fills are persistently worse than the close-proxy backtest, that is **genuine strategy-deployment underperformance**, not a computational false alarm. Keep the original mu0 and sigma immutable; recalibrating them from poor paper results would erase the failure. However, call the alarm **“research-to-paper performance drift”**, not proof that the market regime changed. Show the E8 no-fill/opportunity rate alongside trade-level CUSUM: because the monitor observes **completed positions only**, a strategy whose profitable signals mostly miss may never alarm despite deteriorating net per original opportunity. S1/E6 opportunity-level performance must remain the binding economic review.

## 3. Forecaster 20-outcome blocks — ACCEPT (some evidence)

The original first-60-outcome SD (~0.0001 versus ~0.08 overall) produced **287–503** false alerts in roughly 1,900 sessions. The revised 20-outcome-block SD gives **zero alerts across 95 in-sample blocks** and detects a sustained one-block-SD worsening in roughly **seven blocks**, i.e. ~140 scored outcomes. That is a defensible descriptive monitoring compromise for V6 only, not validated out-of-sample false-alarm control. V1–V3 must remain “not monitored until calibrated”; AI slots using their first 12 live blocks have no monitoring until those blocks finish. Do not introduce a more elaborate test before observing live data. Never turn this warning into a scored failure or forecast suppression.

## 4. Immediate pause versus S1 — ACCEPT (strong evidence on owner decision)

The owner expressly accepted drift pause as a **named exception** to S1's no-statistical-switch-off-before-review rule. The story routes it through `paper -> paused` with approver `drift_rule`, keeps existing positions running to exits and requires an owner restart. This is consistent **only if S1's actual `immediate_pauses` frozen text names that exception before activation**. The pause must apply to the offending registered strategy **across every book**, not globally pause unrelated strategies; “new entries in every book” means no book may use that version. A forecaster alert changes neither scoring nor trades. Test a second unaffected paper version that continues to open while the drifted version is paused.

## 5. What must change before code? — PARTIAL

**Two focused contracts:** (1) block-resampling sensitivity for strategy h, as above; (2) explicit scope and replay of the pause. The story says “new entries in every book pause at once”, which could be read as **all entries in all books**, whereas the implemented D1 transition is **one version**. Clarify: *the alarm pauses only the offending version, in every book using it; unrelated versions remain eligible unless their own hard gates block them*. If the owner instead intends a global book halt, it requires an explicit separate owner decision because it can stop unrelated trades. Test two versions across two books, restart provenance, and a repeated daily run: only the drifted version pauses, one alarm row/alert, open positions still exit. S1 text, D1 schema/transitions, E6 pauser and O1 reviews-step wiring must be merged before any version reaches paper.

**Known limits:** the CUSUM misses slow edge decay; monthly strategies need months to detect a large break; research close proxies and small samples do not guarantee future false-alarm rates. Catastrophic-loss rules remain primary.

## Financial-role challenge

**Mathematician/statistician/quant modeller:** iid false-alarm calibration misses clustered losses and estimates from 30–60 trades are unstable. **F&O/options/volatility trader:** E8 gap/spread deterioration is economically real but no-fill selection is unobserved by a trade-only monitor. **Risk/portfolio/head of desk:** pause scope must be version-specific, with hard-loss brakes unchanged. **Execution/microstructure:** late fills and absent quotes may dominate net returns. **Market/macro/flows/equity research/investment banking:** RBI/Fed/event regimes cluster losses, supporting a block sensitivity. **Regulation:** paper only. **Behavioural:** automatic pause limits loss-chasing but should not be confused with proof of no edge. **Tax/cost accounting:** C1 after-cost nets, not gross or theoretical premiums. **Data engineer/red team:** plant a clustered-loss sample, unrelated second version, duplicate daily run and a delayed exit.

**Final verdict: PARTIAL — proceed with LR3 story implementation once pause scope is unambiguous; calibrate h with a small chronological-block sensitivity before paper activation.** No new drift service, adaptive re-estimation, real orders or extra forecaster scoring gate.
