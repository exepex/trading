## Q1. Is B0 worth one trial? — ACCEPT (some evidence for costs; reasoning only for test value)

Yes **as a measurement, not an edge candidate**. Response 92 reports ₹814–939 round-trip for a four-leg monthly condor, while the historical holdout gross of roughly ₹408 would leave about **−₹406** at the low end of costs. B0's roughly 12 trades per year cannot plausibly confirm a small net edge within S1's decision horizon; it can still test whether real 15:00 quotes, R=60 next-session execution, spread/fees, E7 settlement and ledger reconciliation behave coherently. Count B0 in the trial ledger as the owner decided, but label its results `measurement` in every output and exclude it from G6 promotion and “positive candidate” counts. A severe realised paper loss is operational evidence about the simulator, not a fresh statistical trial proving the short condor profitable.

## Q2. Can a shadow contaminate Book 1? — PARTIAL (strong evidence for current reader risk and proposed fix)

The **actual** current risk is `fno/books/book1_records.py:_chosen`: it selects `chosen_action=trade` from the shared decision directory without checking `mode`. A shadow decision can satisfy that predicate. Current `opening_runs` happens to key on Book 1 fills and shadow trade IDs have a prefix, so a leak into Book 1's P&L is not demonstrated yet. R3's explicit `mode != shadow` filter, own `journal/shadow/<version>` account/terms, and criterion 9's with/without shadow equality tests are the correct small fix.

**Exact test contract:** put one Book 1 chosen trade and one shadow chosen trade on the same session, with **different contract/width/terms and overlapping opportunity IDs**; compare Book 1's opening trades, decided exits, trade counts, E6 scorecard, S2/S3 boards, one-decision finder and actual account files byte-for-byte with the shadow present and absent. Then plant a second shadow with a trade ID not beginning `shadow-`: the mode filter must still isolate it. This tests the root cause, not merely the incidental prefix. C2/C4 and E8 must receive the **shadow's own terms**, not Book 1's; a test with deliberately conflicting risk caps should refuse only the shadow when its own cap is breached.

## Q3. Shadow card delivery time — ACCEPT with scope (reasoning only)

Using the **same actual daily card delivery timestamp T** for the shadow and books, then the same E8 R=60 due snapshot, is a fair common owner-availability counterfactual. It answers: “What would the shadow have filled if the owner received it with the desk's daily card?” It is **not evidence the owner actually received a separate shadow recommendation**, and the card-time dependency means no delivered card => missed shadow opportunity, not a synthetic fill. Do not move T earlier to improve B0's fills. Report the common card time, price-cap refusals and no-fills; when comparing shadow against a book, use the same eligible dates and opportunity denominators.

## Q4. B0 loss above ₹10,000 — PARTIAL (strong evidence for existing prevention; reasoning only for stop policy)

The C2 per-trade maximum-loss gate should refuse any B0 position whose **defined terminal maximum loss plus costs** exceeds ₹10,000 at entry. That prevents an intentionally oversized trade, but cannot guarantee a realised loss never exceeds ₹10,000 if the paper fill/settlement model breaks, an exit is delayed or an accounting correction occurs. E6's alert is useful, and only the owner can retire a research version under the current constitution. **Accept alert + owner retirement for this single measurement shadow**, but make the alert explicitly say **“B0 measurement: realised loss >₹10,000; owner review/retirement required; no further promotion”**, and disclose whether the account remains eligible for further hypothetical entries pending owner action. Do not quietly mark B0 `stopped` without a version/status record. If a repeat occurs in real forward data, escalate the simulator defect before allowing any strategy to use the same fill/settlement path. No new automatic pause framework now.
