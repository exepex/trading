## Q1. One-lot paper pairing and charges — ACCEPT (strong evidence)

Restating every paper leg from its unpadded gross price at the *actual dated one-lot contract size*, subtracting **₹1 per unit per leg on entry and exit**, and recomputing C1 charges at one lot is correct. Do not divide a multi-lot paper net by lots: `fno/costs.py:order_charges_breakdown` charges flat brokerage ₹20 plus 18% GST for each nonzero-turnover order. A two-leg spread entered and exited has four orders: **4 × ₹23.60 = ₹94.40**, independent of the lot count, before turnover-based charges. Dividing a two-lot paper result by two incorrectly assigns ₹47.20. At 65 units, four padded legs/sides cost **4 × 65 × ₹1 = ₹260**; a single ₹65 pad understates the charge by ₹195. Judge 198's SF1/SF2 fixes are sound.

Real-side contract-note charges are the correct ground truth. The residual difference between C1 model charges and real broker charges is not automatically a bias: the binding paired net deliberately asks whether *real* performance matches the fully costed paper model. **Test:** a constructed 2-lot paper/1-lot real spread with equal gross prices, four flat brokerage charges, turnover charges and pad must reproduce the difference to the paisa; also include a settlement leg (C1's `settlement_charges_breakdown` differs from a normal closing order). Do not silently apply a fictitious closing trade charge when E7 settles a leg.

## Q2. Screenshot and five-minute quote — PARTIAL, non-blocking with a concrete capture check (some evidence)

The screenshot's **visible phone clock** plus typed time is a pragmatic manual source, because Drive upload can follow the trade. The stored file SHA and optional filename timestamp mismatch alert are useful, but they cannot independently prove the screenshot was taken at the typed instant. The more common operational error is the owner taking a screenshot, spending several minutes placing a multi-leg order, then reporting the *screenshot* time as the *order* time. A 10:00 quote for a 10:07 order is seven minutes old, not zero. This can understate adverse slippage and alter the ₹2/₹1.50 gates.

**Small exact check:** store **two separate times** per leg: screenshot capture time from the image and the broker order-submission time from the order confirmation/contract note (typed by the owner). Age = order time minus quote observation time; negative age or >5 minutes is **unknown**, not a valid slippage observation. For an A16 fallback, use the latest **observed fetch time ≤ the actual order time**, not the nominal 15-minute mark. A screenshot taken 10:00 with order at 10:04 is eligible; order at 10:07 is unknown. The RM2 story already asks for order time and image time; add an explicit test preventing them from being silently conflated. This is an ordinary human-order timing risk, not a request for OCR or broker API integration.

## Q3. Stops and pass — PARTIAL: one decision-changing gap (strong evidence on frozen text and RM2 flow)

The thresholds match the owner-decided gate: cumulative net **≤ −₹20,000**; drawdown from peak **≥ ₹20,000**; five consecutive *exited* trades' mean adverse execution slippage **>₹2/unit**; at pass, ≥max(30, registered) trades and ≥6 months, paired one-sided 95% lower bound **≥ −₹500/trade**, prediction floor, mean slippage **≤₹1.50/unit**, unknown legs **≤10%**, and placed cards **≥90%**. The story's boundary examples are arithmetically correct: a peak of +₹6,000 falling to −₹14,000 is exactly ₹20,000 drawdown. Missing slippage remains unknown, not zero.

**CHANGE — late or missing real exit reports can bypass a stop.** RM2 says the owner reports each exit *after* the fill and that stops are evaluated **after every recorded exit**. It does not specify what happens if an actual exit occurred but its record is delayed, or if a trade is still open while another entry is considered. A late report of a −₹21,000 closed trade could allow the owner to enter another trade before RM2 recognises the stop. This is a normal manual-reporting failure, and it changes a real-money stop. **Minimal rule:** RM2's status must be **`records_pending — no further test trade`** whenever an entered real trade has no reported exit/settlement by its registered due exit time, or when the owner reports an execution but the Drive write fails. Before any subsequent real-test card is treated as eligible to place, reconcile outstanding exits and evaluate all newly recorded stops in chronological **actual exit-time** order. If a historical stop threshold was crossed, mark the test stopped effective at that exit, flag any intervening real trades as protocol breaches, and do not count them toward a pass. This is a reporting/status gate and explicit instruction to the owner, **not** an automated order block. Add a fixture: one exit at 10:00 with −₹21,000 reported at 15:00, another eligible card at 12:00; the 12:00 card must be flagged `do not place / unreconciled`, and the delayed report must mark the stop at 10:00. A real-money test cannot promise automatic protection when the owner does not report fills; the owner must also follow this instruction.

**Also test:** an unknown leg does not silently become zero in the five-trade slippage stop. The frozen gate permits excluding unknown legs, but the count and missingness must be shown alongside the computed mean; the ≥10% unknown rule applies to **pass**, not retroactively to the stop.

## Q4. First mark strictly after paper fill — ACCEPT (strong evidence)

Reading that same snapshot as the “next” observation would produce a tautological zero for a price that is already the quote used to fill. **Strictly after** correctly chooses 10:30, subject to an actual valid quote for the contract.
