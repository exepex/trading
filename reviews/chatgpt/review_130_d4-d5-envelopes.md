# Review 130 — D4 Book 2 envelope and D5 Book 3 constitution

**Scope:** `story/d4-d5-envelopes`, Response 210, Response 204, Judge 207 and addendum, D4/D5 story text, design 12.2–12.4, design v2 §3.2 and owner decisions of 16 Oct. **Second reviewer:** ChatGPT under quality rule 9. **Overall: PARTIAL — accept the restricted first version for implementation, with one attribution rule to pin before counted Book 2 comparisons.** Evidence labels follow AGENTS.md. This is a story-text review, not an implementation test; Judge 207's PASS is reported evidence. No real orders.

## 1. Is Book 2 envelope v1 too narrow? — PARTIAL (strong evidence on available actions; reasoning only on usefulness)

With one approved strategy and one lot, `reduce_lots` is a no-op, `choose_strategy` has only one choice, and `follow_rule` is the default. Thus AI's **effective interventions are veto/no-trade and choosing among the strategy's registered widths**. That is a valid *narrow* question: can AI avoid poor rule entries or choose a better defined-risk width? It is **not** evidence that general AI judgement adds value across strikes, expiries, structures or exits. The design acknowledges those actions are unsupported by D1/E4/S3, so adding them now would be scope creep. **ACCEPT envelope v1 for a first experiment** if reports label the estimand `AI veto/width selection within the same registered playbook`, report veto rate and width changes, and keep the rule's default as a paired control. A zero-difference run is inconclusive, not proof that AI adds no value.

## 2. Can Book 3 game the constitution? — PARTIAL (strong evidence on registered-before timing; reasoning only on multiplicity)

A spec registered on an earlier session with a strict ancestor commit prevents **same-day** invention after seeing that day's prices. It does **not** prevent registering 20 near-identical strategies yesterday, choosing whichever seems attractive today, then counting the winning one as if only one hypothesis had been tried. This is a realistic adaptive-selection path once Book 3's strategy factory operates. **Do not add an arbitrary cap or minimum waiting days:** both would be easy to game and could exclude useful ideas. Instead, before any Book 3 strategy earns a confirmatory claim, bind every registered variant to its existing **G0–G6 idea/trial ledger and cumulative research-question family** (design v2 §3.1). Selection among registered strategies may continue for paper exploration, but the scoreboard must count the trials and evaluate the *predeclared selection policy* as a version, not a cherry-picked survivor. This is an existing provenance/multiplicity contract, not a new registry. Test: two prior-session variants, one chosen after market data, must not yield a single-trial confirmatory pass. The owner can still see exploratory returns.

## 3. Does the traded version's envelope leave a gap? — PARTIAL (strong evidence on story rule; reasoning only on authority escalation)

D4's rule that the **actually traded** version's `trade_envelope:<name>-v<n>` governs `choose_strategy` is deterministic and avoids a book-level envelope collision. But consider rule-default A with `choose_strategy=forbidden`, while version B allows it. An AI could use B's permission to switch away from A. That may be exactly the intended policy, but only if B was an **eligible, pre-registered candidate in the day's D2 set** and its envelope was frozen before the decision. **Exact condition for slice E:** C4 must verify that the chosen version was in the same day's eligible candidate set, that its registered envelope hash matches D6/D3 `served`, and that the action is permitted under that traded version. If an action is shadow-only, record but do not score it. A simple two-version fixture (A forbidden, B allowed) should show B can be chosen **only when B is eligible and registered**. Do not invent a “default version must also permit” rule unless the owner explicitly wants a stricter policy; it would contradict the selected traded-version contract.

## 4. Monthly expiry and 21-day exit — ACCEPT (strong evidence for data scope; reasoning only for generalisation)

A4/E4/S3 currently cover monthly NIFTY quotes, and D1 only implements a time exit. Restricting Book 3 to monthly expiries and planned exits within **21 calendar days** is a coherent research universe. It is not the entire NIFTY F&O opportunity set: weekly expiry, shorter intraday responses, discretionary stops and other underlyings are untested. That limits what the whole-system question can claim, but does not make the test invalid. Label results `monthly NIFTY defined-risk strategies with ≤21-day planned time exits`; do not infer performance for all F&O or fast event trading. A new universe requires a new registered constitution version and data coverage.

## 5. Must anything change before slice E? — PARTIAL

**Two minimal contracts to pin in slice E's implementation tests:** (1) the traded version is registered, actually eligible that day and its hash is checked independently by C4; (2) Book 3's adaptive strategy variants remain visible in the existing G0–G6 cumulative trial family, and only a frozen selection policy can make a confirmatory claim. These can change whether a trade is allowed or whether a strategy passes; they are not speculative safety infrastructure. The D3 refusal of any non-empty `owner_override` is already an owner decision and must be implemented before Book 2/3 paper decisions. No new waiting-period cap, strike chooser or exit engine now.

## Financial-role cross-check

**Mathematician/statistician/quant modeller:** an adaptive winner from 20 variants is not a one-test edge; define the actual narrow comparison. **F&O trader/options strategist/volatility trader:** width and veto can matter, but missing strike/expiry/exit freedom limits economic opportunities. **Risk/portfolio/head of desk:** C2/C4 stay independent hard gates, and no owner override may weaken them. **Market research/macro/derivatives flow/equity research/investment banking:** macro/news-driven vetoes are a legitimate narrow intervention but not proof of broad AI skill. **Execution/microstructure:** monthly quotes and one time exit limit realistic fill inference. **Regulation:** paper only, no broker orders. **Behavioural:** resist promoting the best variant after trying many. **Tax/cost accounting:** compare after-cost outcomes on the same opportunity dates. **Data engineer/red team:** plant unregistered B, stale envelope hash, shadow-only action and 20 lookalike specs.

**Final verdict: PARTIAL, suitable for story-text approval with the two slice-E tests/claim contracts above.** No demonstrated normal-running defect justifies broadening the constitution or envelope now. No profitable edge is established.
