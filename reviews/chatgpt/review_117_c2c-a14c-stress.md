## Q1. Stress the exposure as well as the scan? — ACCEPT (strong evidence for arithmetic; some evidence for economic sizing)

**Yes.** Response 77 measured the maximum **whole-margin** rise, whereas the existing C2/C4 formula stressed only scan. On the 14 Oct SF 100 row: broker basis ₹59,899.32, scan-only stress ₹60,269.33 (just **0.62%**), whole-margin stress ₹86,854.01 (about **45%** above broker basis). BF 300 goes from ₹59,077.07 to ₹85,661.75. This is the exposure-dominated, observed situation the owner intends to cover; stressing only scan cannot implement the measured 1.45 buffer.

**Qualification:** exposure is an exchange/broker collateral charge, not a mathematical terminal payoff loss. Index changes and NSE circulars can increase it. A single k on the measured whole margin is a conservative **capital reservation**, not proof that worst realised loss is 45% higher. Do not multiply the paid premium again. A paper strategy may be refused more often, which is an intended trade-changing effect.

## Q2. CCS/PCS cap and stressed exposure? — ACCEPT (strong evidence for worked CCS; reasoning only for the boundary)

**Keep the STR1 cap on the SPAN component and the broker-floor requirement; do not cap exposure at terminal payoff loss.** The cap represents a defined-risk spread's terminal payoff; the exchange can require collateral exceeding that payoff. CCS 300 (65 units): scan ₹9,429.55, NOV −₹11,306.75, exposure ₹29,538.53. The broker SPAN component ₹20,736.30 exceeds the payoff-derived cap ₹8,193.25, so it stays at the broker floor. The proposed total is

`20,736.30 − 11,306.75 + 1.45 × 29,538.53 = ₹52,260.42`.

The unstressed ₹38,968.08 and unconstrained k-multiple ₹56,503.72 bracket this value. **Do not “fix” the apparent excess over terminal max loss by lowering broker collateral.** C2 and independent C4 must match to the paisa for this row and planted PCS/CCS rows. The owner's ₹10k terminal-loss cap remains a *separate* gate.

## Q3. Historical maximum, 21-day exit, 0.05 ceiling? — PARTIAL (some evidence)

**ACCEPT** the fixed maximum across every entry session since 1 Jan 2020, all registered structures/widths, revalued daily until the **predeclared 21-days-before-expiry exit**, rounded **up** in 0.05 steps. A quantile would knowingly discard the very crisis observation the owner wants as a capital buffer; per-structure k adds complexity and allows one structure to consume a smaller cushion without evidence of superior calibration. The three historical anchors (1.127 in 2026, 1.265 in 2020, 1.407 in 2024) are **reported observations**, not a verified full-history maximum. Full 2020–26 results may legitimately exceed 1.45.

**CHANGE — ordinary data completeness (strong evidence on contract):** “archive covering fewer sessions than last run” is not sufficient. A weekly archive can have **the same session count but a different missing date**, and A14c excludes any holding with a missing daily i1. That can remove the previous worst observation and lower the computed factor; a held decrease protects the active value, but a different missing date can also suppress a **new increase** and silently leave the buffer too small. Before accepting a weekly recompute, verify the actual required regular-session **date set** from 2020 through the last complete session (not only its count); name gaps and refuse to append a normal applied row while required dates are missing. Use the existing A9 manifest/calendar; no new service. Test same-count/different-day archive and a missing date inside the peak holding. **This changes entry eligibility**, so it is not a speculative guard.

**Known limit:** the 21-day exit horizon does not cover later holds or an emergency NSE rate change; such cases need a separately registered method, not retroactive reinterpretation of k.

## Q4. Weekly automatic file, monotonic factor, Groww check? — PARTIAL (strong evidence on point-in-time semantics; some evidence on live broker matching)

**ACCEPT C2d's append-only dated file**, seed 14 Oct 2026 at 1.45, C2/C4 selecting the latest `as_of <= decision day`, 30-day expiry, no factor below 1, and removal of the duplicate factor from `config.yaml`. Example: with rows 14 Oct=1.45 and 8 Nov=1.50, a 7 Nov decision/replay must use **1.45**, and 8 Nov uses **1.50**. A held row carries the **old effective factor**, not the newly computed lower value. A rise applies automatically; **every fall is held** pending an actual owner decision. Weekly Sunday is sensible: three missed weekly runs remain before the 30-day expiry forces a refusal. Use the **last fully archived session** rather than pretending Sunday's i1 file exists.

**CHANGE — normal source-data failure (strong evidence on the stated first-seen contract):** ensure the append step fails closed when the latest **expected** NSE session is not archived yet, even if its previous weekly date set is intact. A job that silently computes through an older last day can append a fresh `as_of` date, resetting the 30-day age while using stale margin data. Reuse the date-set completeness check above and record `coverage_through` in the evidence/report (it need not be a new config field). Test an archive ending one session early on Sunday: no fresh effective row, one alert; the old row ages normally. **This can permit a paper entry that should have been refused**, so it matters.

**ACCEPT the owner's Groww comparison as an alert-only diagnostic**, with SPAN tolerance ₹1 and exposure tolerance 1% **after index-base rescaling**, for the same SF 100 basket, contracts and lot size. Response 77's 14 Oct example matches Groww's SPAN ₹6,893 to NSE ₹6,893.25 and exposure ₹58,819.02 at Groww's 15:32 index base; the earlier NSE base gives ₹59,077.07. One basket does not validate every structure or future circular. A mismatch must visibly name the broker and NSE figures and trigger a manual margin-source review; under the owner's explicit decision it need not automatically lower/hold k. This is an accepted limitation, not an excuse to hide the alert.

## Q5. What must change before code? — PARTIAL

**Two small safeguards, one root cause:** check the **actual session-date coverage**, including the latest expected completed session, before computing/appending the weekly factor. Both prevent a fresh-looking factor from masking incomplete data. Add two planted tests: (1) same session count but a missing/replaced date, (2) latest session missing while the previous archive is complete. The run must refuse to append an `applied` row and alert. All other stated tests remain appropriate: independent C2/C4 arithmetic, capped CCS 300 fixture, historical anchors, incremental-vs-full equality, held fall, factor-file replay and age expiry.

**Implementation order:** C2c (formula) → C2d (single dated factor source) → A14c (weekly producer). Freeze the seed, verify the first historical recomputation and the owner decisions before the first automatic row. Do not introduce a quantile estimator, separate per-structure factors, a broker-key GitHub job or automatic config edits.
