## 3. Slippage base and timing — PARTIAL (strong evidence for distinct price/time definitions; reasoning only for solution)

**ACCEPT** comparing real fills to the *un-padded* paper touch (ask for buys, bid for sells); using the padded paper fill would grant another pad of slack. `fno/desk/execution.py:FillMaker.fill` passes `Order(quote.side, quote.price, quote.units, FILL_PAD_RS)`, confirming price and pad are distinct.

**CHANGE:** a real fill executed sooner than E8's **R=60-minute, often next-session** paper fill is not a clean execution-slippage measurement. Example: paper buy ask ₹100 at next-session 10:15; owner buys at ₹110 at 15:15 on the previous session; the proposed rule calls that +₹10 adverse “slippage” even though it is predominantly a different market time. Reverse the overnight move and it falsely rewards execution. This can decide both the pass and the five-trade stop. **Small fix:** keep E8 R=60 as the economic paper counterfactual (do not rewrite S1), but compute the execution-quality slippage separately against the **first independently recorded contemporaneous valid ask/bid at the owner's actual order time**. If no such quote exists, mark the slippage criterion **not met/unknown**, not zero. Report the E8 timing gap separately and compare the paired real-versus-E8 net including that gap. A16's 15-minute observations are not always simultaneous with the owner's order; freeze an allowed quote-age rule before the test. This requires a recorded quote at execution time, not a second automated order system.

**Also CHANGE:** the design calls `real_test_pass` a “paired test,” but its binding net criterion is **unpaired**: `paper_mean - t × paper_SD × sqrt(1/n+1/m)` forecasts an independent new sample, not the paired real-minus-paper differences from the *same cards*. The paired mean/CI is only reported, not judged. That is not identical to the owner's accepted paired-test requirement in §8.4. **Exact fix:** keep the one-sided prediction lower bound as a required safety floor, **and** make the paired real-minus-paper net test a binding criterion with its sample, one-sided interval and a predeclared maximum acceptable deterioration (recommend lower one-sided 95% bound of paired mean difference ≥ −₹500 per trade at 1 lot, inclusive of charges). Report missed-card selection separately; do not claim 20 paired trades prove equivalence if its interval is too wide. This extra criterion is my proposal requiring owner approval, not an existing decided threshold.

## 4. Completeness of seven conditions and real test — PARTIAL (strong evidence for arithmetic, some evidence for replay feasibility)

**ACCEPT** the seven named conditions, the 2020 (20 Feb–30 Apr) and 2024 (3–7 Jun) windows, the 1-lot test, the ₹20,000 cumulative-loss stop, and the owner approval. The interval `paper_mean − t(0.95,m−1) × s × sqrt(1/n+1/m)` is the usual one-sided prediction bound **for the mean of n independent future observations under an iid normal model**; it is not generally calibrated for serially dependent or heavy-tailed option returns. Call it a model-based prediction bound, not a distribution-free guarantee. With n=m=20 and s=₹5,000, its half-width is about ₹2,158 (t19≈1.729): a wide and weak filter. The `0.05/m` threshold is Bonferroni (also Holm's first step), not a full step-down Holm implementation; this conservative one-step bound is valid for a **fixed family and one predeclared look**. The trial count must be frozen at the confirmatory review, with all registered ideas included; repeated rechecks require their own error control. Route (a) is preferable.

**Real cap arithmetic:** ₹5,000 / 65 = **₹76.92 per NIFTY unit before fees**, not after fees. For a defined-risk credit vertical, maximum net loss per lot is `65 × (width − net credit per unit) + entry/exit charges`. At width 100, with ₹30 credit and ₹200 charges, loss = ₹4,750 and fits; at width 200 with the same credit/charges, loss = ₹11,250 and fails. The test must use the actual dated contract lot and bid/ask, not assume all 20 past quotes describe one unchanged expiry. NSE's 2025 circular revised NIFTY to 65 and its contract specification directs users to current permitted lots; confirm again before a real trade (official NSE circular FAOP/70616 and contract specification).

**CHANGE:** `real_cap_fit` says “every one” of the last 20 sessions' quotes must fit. A strategy only trades on **eligible signals**; requiring cap fit on 20 arbitrary non-signal days can wrongly reject a viable strategy or admit one whose actual signalled trades exceed the cap. Replace with the **last 20 eligible entry opportunities** (or all if fewer, then not met), using their actual chosen contracts, lots, premiums, charges and time-valid quotes; also require the next proposed real trade to pass the cap with current prices. This changes eligibility and therefore needs fixing before freeze. A missing quote is not a zero-cost pass.

**CHANGE:** `fill_honesty` compares paper touch with the **first snapshot after the fill**; a later ask/bid movement is not evidence that the earlier paper touch was fictitious. For example, paper buy ask ₹100 at 10:15 and next A16 ask ₹110 at 10:30 => apparent +₹10 “paper kindness” despite a legitimate earlier ask. Use the snapshot that actually underlies the E8 fill (already hashed) to verify the paper touch/quote age and depth, and compare to an independent contemporaneous quote only where one exists; otherwise report unknown. This is an actual timing-design defect capable of refusing/approving a strategy for the wrong reason.

**Crash replay:** settlement-only data do not reproduce intraday fills, legging or the precise 21-day exit price. The gate appropriately fails closed on unavailable data, but must label the 2020/2024 result as a **daily-settlement proxy** and not infer an executable 1-lot net loss cap without a predeclared conservative slippage/charge model. This is a known limitation, not a request to build intraday history.
