## Q1. Declared cost split — PARTIAL (strong evidence on code; reasoning only on allocation fairness)

**ACCEPT the declared upper-bound allocation rather than adding an unmeasured decider share.** `cost.py:book_day_paise` takes F8's book allocation and prorates by that day's recorded minutes; `split_paise` allocates it equally across `consulted_units`, with a deterministic paisa remainder. F8 records one book session, not per-subagent minutes, so any “decider's share” would be invented. This deliberately biases against `earns` and toward `drop proposed`; label it a **conservative cost allocation**, not measured marginal savings from removing the role. The owner decides any drop.

**CHANGE one ordinary reporting inconsistency:** `report.py:build` calculates `cost_per_opportunity` from *all consulted days* in the selected period, including `shadow_unavailable`, open and otherwise unscored days, but `score_unit` uses only completed, scored ablation opportunities. The resulting cost average can differ from the cost of the days whose paired nets were actually tested. **Worked example:** one unit consulted on two days with allocated shares ₹100 and ₹300; only the ₹300 day has a closed pair. The tested net is for the ₹300 day but the code subtracts ₹200. That ₹100 difference can change an earns/drop decision near zero. **Small fix:** for the scored sample, average the role's allocated daily cost over the **same scored applicable dates** as its net series, while reporting the separate whole-period consulted-day allocation as an operating-cost disclosure. If S1 explicitly intends a whole-period operating cost instead, keep the existing formula but freeze that estimand and label it, never call it “cost on scored opportunities.” Add a two-day fixture with one excluded pair and unequal daily costs. This is a real normal-path category: F9a explicitly excludes open/unavailable pairs.

## Q2. Holm and 30 changed opportunities — ACCEPT (strong evidence on code; reasoning only on power)

`verdict.py` enters **both** one-sided p-values for every eligible unit in one Holm family. This correctly prevents choosing a direction after observing results and avoids multiple false drops across roles. With one unit, the smallest p must be **<0.025**, not 0.05. That is intentionally stricter than a single one-sided 95% interval; the printed 5th/95th percentiles are descriptive and must not override Holm. The code requires at least 30 **changed** opportunities, not 30 calendar days. With six rotating units and one eligible changed opportunity per cycle, 30 changes need at least 180 book-decision days; if changes are rare it can take much longer. This is an honest minimum for provisional decisions, **not a statistical guarantee of adequate power**. Keep “inconclusive” rather than lowering it after seeing outcomes.

## Q3. Twenty applicable-opportunity blocks — PARTIAL (strong evidence on implementation; reasoning only on regime coverage)

`score.py:_series` puts scored opportunities in date order; S3's `resampled_ratios` uses 20 consecutive **observations**, not 20 consecutive exchange sessions. With six units, one block may span roughly 120 trading sessions. This preserves the ordering of observed ablations but is **not** the same dependence model as S3's daily 20-session blocks. A real regime change within 120 sessions can change the mix of observations; no simulation establishes nominal coverage for these sparse blocks.

**No extra bootstrap service now:** print the calendar span of the actual 20-observation blocks and the sample's year/month distribution beside the p-values; mark claims provisional until forward observations are available. Do not assert that the block bootstrap is calibrated on sparse role samples. If the owner's intended interpretation is 20 **calendar** sessions, change the resampling unit before S1 freeze, rather than silently inheriting S3's meaning.

**Positive:** `checks.py:check_hashes` compares the real/shadow pack hashes, their snapshot IDs, and the run-log snapshot; `valuation.py:value_pair` waits if either side is open and treats an unfilled take as zero; `report.py` lists excluded and waiting rows. The two Judge 194 follow-ups address real cases: an unfilled take versus a skip counts as a changed decision even when both nets are zero, and pilot rows do not advance the ablation rotation.
