## Q2. Study 3's test and Holm — ACCEPT with a statistical qualification (strong evidence on code; some evidence on empirical failure)

`study3.py:member_p` returns batch-means p when it already exceeds Holm's first step; otherwise it runs `confirmatory_test` and uses the **maximum** of batch-means and bootstrap p. This shortcut is exact for the binary question “does any member pass the first Holm step?” If the minimum p exceeds α/3 no claim exists; if at least one passes, a claim exists. The shortcut does **not** calculate the number or identities of *all* Holm rejections after the first step, but Study 3's specified outcome is **any false claim**, so this is not a defect. Shared 63-session block indices and per-population centering are visible in `block_paths`, `population_paths`. The Gaussian AR(1) fixture and alternate-seed judge check support implementation fidelity.

**Qualification:** “No rate passes” is established **under the chosen historical bootstrap null**, not for every possible data-generating process. It is nevertheless the correct fail-closed decision. At α=0.02 the observed -d any-look false-claim rate **0.136** versus permitted **0.045**; at α=0.005 it is **0.071**, still above **0.045**. Those gaps are too large to dismiss as Monte Carlo noise with 2,000 paths.

## Q4. Long memory versus skew; alternatives — PARTIAL (some evidence for dependence, reasoning only for future tests)

I agree that **longer-than-five-session dependence is a major demonstrated mechanism**, not that it has been causally isolated as *the* cause. The two market pairs have 63-vs-5-session variance ratios **1.43** and **2.08**, and the sign autocorrelation remains about **0.2 at lag 20**; the pair with ratio **0.62** shows little over-rejection. This association strongly supports inadequate dependence handling. But the observed daily-difference skew **+1.83/+1.58** and crash-day concentration can also distort the studentized test, especially in small effective samples. The experiments have not factorially isolated dependence from skew; say **“dependence is the leading supported explanation, with skew/tails contributing.”**

**Alternative to investigate, not to adopt:** a predeclared **HAC/Newey–West long-run variance** test with lag bandwidth around **21–42 sessions**, using a one-sided statistic for the daily cross-sectional mean, and a **stationary bootstrap** with mean block length selected from pre-freeze autocorrelation diagnostics. Test both against the exact 2024 and 2025 nulls, both signs, two looks, Holm K=3 and the same 0.025/0.045 limits. A HAC estimator can be less sample-hungry computationally, but **cannot create independent regimes**; there is no evidence it will control size on 437 sessions or outperform 21-session blocks. If it fails, remain descriptive-only. Do not retrofit a new threshold to the observed BR1 outcome.

## Q5. Descriptive-only communication — PARTIAL (strong evidence on wording; reasoning only on reader confusion)

The protocol's opening says **DRAFT, NOT FROZEN, DESCRIPTIVE ONLY** and `looks` says none; `claim_meaning` prohibits wording as proven skill or trading profit. That is appropriate. But an unqualified **“95% interval”** next to a result can be mistaken for calibrated inferential coverage even though the historical test failed size checks. **Exact wording to add to every chart/table and export:** “**Exploratory estimate; interval is descriptive and its coverage is not validated for these dependent market days. Not evidence of forecast skill, trading profit or real-money eligibility.**” Keep the negative results and all baseline comparisons visible; do not mark a family `PASS` or `beats baseline`. Annual null rechecks are exploratory monitoring, never retroactive approval; only a new pre-registered version can claim.
