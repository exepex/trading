## 2. Gap attribution: reproducible but incorrectly classified

**Perspectives:** mathematician, execution, data engineer.
**Confidence: strong evidence from source inspection and independent recalculation.**

The code sets `FIRST_BAR_END = time(9, 25)`, so it includes second-bar exits at 09:25. It then uses `candles[i - 1]` as the previous close. For a 09:25 exit that is the same morning's first bar, not the previous session's close.

Exactly two entries are affected:

- **E126, ICICIBANK, 2026-09-07 09:25 IST**
- **E190, TCS, 2026-10-12 09:25 IST**

Both sessions contain a 09:15 first bar, ending at 09:20. Therefore these are genuinely second-bar exits, not missing-opening-bar cases.

There are **25 true first-bar stops**, not 27.

### Independently recalculated bridges

|---|---:|---:|
| P&L before previous session close | -₹6,584.81 | -₹8,951.19 |
| Previous-session close to session open | -₹85,518.63 | -₹87,596.81 |
| Session open to exit | -₹37,932.93 | -₹41,736.24 |
| Charges | -₹1,565.30 | -₹1,707.05 |
| Total net | **-₹131,601.67** | **-₹139,991.29** |

Individually rounded component cells can differ by a cent from the rounded unrounded total.

The main net result is unchanged. What changes is the explanation of where this group's losses arose.

**Required correction — strong evidence:** either restrict the group to actual first-bar exits and use the first column, or label the 27 entries as opening-ten-minute stops and use the second column. Always obtain the prior session close and actual session opening bar explicitly. If opening data are missing, mark the attribution incomplete rather than treating a later available candle as the true open.

Also call the overnight bucket an **overnight option-value change**. Because model time advances, it includes time decay as well as underlying-price movement; it is not an isolated causal estimate of the price gap alone.

This is a functional attribution error, not a formatting concern.

## 5. Skew does not automatically make a debit put spread dearer

**Perspective:** options strategist.
**Confidence: strong conditional pricing reasoning; real-market outcome remains unmeasured.**

The spread buys one put and sells another. Its debit depends on both prices, not "put IV" in isolation.

Using the same model at spot 22,370 and 37 days:

- Long 22,350 put IV = 15%; short 22,300 put IV = 15%: debit **₹1,413.90**.
- Keep long IV at 15%; increase the sold lower-strike IV to **15.01%**: debit **₹1,395.90**.

A richer sold wing can cheapen the spread. An increase in the bought leg can do the opposite. Therefore, "extra put skew may make the spread not fit" is possible but not established by the direction of skew alone. Measure both executable premiums and ensure the price surface respects no-arbitrage bounds.

### Q3. Is max(₹0.50, 1% of premium) per leg an acceptable fill allowance?

**Accept only as a labelled screening/stress assumption, not a validated execution-cost estimate. Confidence: strong evidence for its numerical effect; reasoning only for the screening grid.**

Define whether it is a full bid/ask width or a **one-way adverse fill pad**. They are different. If a full width is given, the buy/sell adjustment around a midpoint is ordinarily half that width before additional slippage.

Under the one-way-pad interpretation, four executed orders at lot 65 have a minimum extra cost of:

`4 × 65 × ₹0.50 = ₹130`.

For the 12% IV, 50-point bull call example:

| Underlying move | Net without fill padding | Aggregate pad cost before fee recalculation | Net with max(₹0.50, 1% premium) padding, fees recomputed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Flat | -₹245.86 | ₹948.49 | **-₹1,193.66** |
| +1% | +₹140.93 | ₹1,129.01 | **-₹987.25** |
| +2% | +₹465.42 | ₹1,343.38 | **-₹876.96** |

Pads are calculated on each leg's own model price, at entry and exit. Charges are then recalculated from padded execution prices. These results remain before operating bills and income-tax effects.

The relative allowance applies to the **large gross premiums of both legs**, not the small net spread debit. It can dominate the result. This is not proof that real costs are that high or low; it demonstrates why an unmeasured assumption must not be presented as execution evidence.

**Recommended screening protocol — reasoning only:**

1. Show fixed one-way pads of **₹0.50, ₹1 and ₹2 per leg per executed order**, plus the proposed relative-price stress.
2. Report profit and admission sensitivity and the break-even pad; do not call any grid value measured or guaranteed conservative.
3. Collect synchronized bid/ask prices, quote sizes, timestamps and observed fill/latency evidence for selected contracts.
4. Calibrate the base execution assumption to those observations; keep a predeclared worse-cost stress.
5. Use completed t signals and next-session close **proxies** only in the daily screen. These proxy results remain insufficient for executable-policy sign-off.
