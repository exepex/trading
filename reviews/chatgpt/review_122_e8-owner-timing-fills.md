## 1. Price-cap adverse selection — ACCEPT, with a precise scoreboard denominator (some evidence; strong reasoning)

**ACCEPT the price cap as the pending recommended entry rule.** A limit order does not fill on a morning when the ask for a bought option has become too expensive, but it may fill after an adverse underlying move when that option has become cheaper. E8a reports mean absolute long-option price movement of about **52–55 points** (roughly ₹3,400–3,600 per 65-unit lot) between a 15:00 decision and next-session R=60, with moves exceeding 10% of trade risk on **23–28%** of observations. Those are candle-last-trade *proxies*, not evidence of fillable morning bid/ask, and cannot quantify the actual fraction refused by a price cap.

**The report must show BOTH:** (i) net P&L **per executed fill** and (ii) net P&L **per eligible original opportunity**, where a missed entry has zero realised P&L but remains in the denominator and in the fill-rate count. Also report the miss reasons and NIFTY move split on the entire original opportunity set. A no-fill is not a losing trade, and a hypothetical “would have won” fill at an unapproved price is not realised P&L. Do not drop missed opportunities from the comparison or call the conditional filled sample representative. The E8 story already reports fills/misses and the move split; this is a **reporting definition** for BR/S1/S2, not a new execution engine. **Verdict: ACCEPT** the design with that interpretation.

## 2. Availability calendar privacy and time witness — ACCEPT (strong evidence on contract; reasoning only on privacy)

A public file containing **dates and available/away only**, without location, reason, contact details or itinerary, is sufficient for this personal paper system. E8 rule 8 requires the `away` row's Git commit to predate T; that prevents retrospective deletion of unfavourable opportunities. Moving it to Drive plus a separately committed hash adds synchronization and does not improve the privacy of dates already visible in a public commit. The owner should knowingly accept that public away dates reveal a limited absence pattern; if that is unacceptable, choose Drive **before collecting rows**, not after using outcomes. **No new cryptographic service.** A planted late `away` row must be ignored, as E8 AC7 already requires.

## 3. One available session for entry; exits never expire — ACCEPT (reasoning only, supported by the normal clock)

At O1's actual **15:15-or-later card**, R=5/30/60 all reach the **next session** at 09:30/09:45/10:15, not the same day. A further away session would turn a one-day-old signal into a two-day-old one. **ACCEPT** refusing that entry as `missed, owner away` instead of chasing the signal days later. Existing positions are different: exits must stay pending until a valid due quote or E7 expiry settlement, without inventing a fill. Report missed/away entries and exit delay separately. **Known limit:** a persistent outage can postpone an exit and increase loss; do not treat a pending exit as a successful risk reduction.

## 4. Four views and possible verdict contamination — ACCEPT with one concrete integration test (strong evidence on stated isolation; reasoning only on future implementation)

The four views are **counterfactuals of one decision, not four independent trials**. E8 rule 10 says C2, E6, S1 and S2 read only `verdict` (R=60) for binding risk/performance; `r5`, `r30` and `e4_1515` are diagnostic accounts with independent exits. That is the right architecture. The first view can fill next morning while `e4_1515` fills before the card is actually delivered; the latter must be explicitly labelled an **unexecutable timing yardstick**, not an owner's achieved fill.

**Exact test:** arrange a fixture in which `e4_1515` profits and `verdict` misses or loses, then assert that the book's available cash, open-risk caps, drawdown brake and S1 pass/stop verdict are identical whether the diagnostic views are enabled or not. Also test the reverse. The story's AC9 checks C2 only; extend that fixture to the **S1 stop** path because that is the consequential output. No second scoring framework is needed.

## 5. AnyPrice, approved cash and maximum loss — CHANGE (strong evidence for separate risk/funds concepts; reasoning only on choosing the option)

**Do not approve `AnyPrice` as written without an available-funds check.** Rule 6 and AC6 say `AnyPrice` may fill far worse than the approved entry so long as its recomputed ₹10,000 maximum loss and buckets pass. But the decision's **approved cash** is not the same thing as hard maximum loss or available cash. A long call can have a premium debit of ₹8,000 at decision time and ₹9,500 at the due mark: both are under a ₹10,000 maximum-loss cap (before charges), yet if the verdict account has only ₹9,000 available, the latter cannot be funded. For a vertical, broker margin and the net premium can change independently of terminal max loss. This is a normal morning repricing path, not a theoretical adversarial edit.

**Small exact correction:** every entry-range option, including `AnyPrice`, must **re-run the existing C2/C4 entry funds, broker/stressed-margin and risk checks on the actual due-mark bid/ask, current verdict account and pending fills** before writing any entry. If insufficient cash/margin, refuse `missed, insufficient funds`; if over loss/bucket cap, refuse `missed, over risk cap`. The *old approved entry price* need not bind `AnyPrice`, but **current cash and margin must**. Add one AC6 fixture for a bought option whose new debit is below the loss cap but above available cash, and one credit vertical with adequate max-loss headroom but inadequate margin. This does not require a new guard or service: reuse the existing entry validation.

**Owner choice:** recommend **`ApprovedLimit`**, not `AnyPrice`, for the initial frozen S1 protocol (reasoning only). It matches the owner's quoted limit, avoids arbitrary repricing, and makes adverse selection visible through opportunity-level reporting. A ±0.5% band is not supported by a measured incremental net benefit: the E8a study shows **204/645 = 31.6%** SF and **210/660 = 31.8%** LC overnight cases would be excluded by that band alone (some evidence). Do not add it now. The owner must decide before S1's freeze, as Judge 193 addendum 3 requires.

## Role-by-role financial check

**Mathematician/statistician/quant modeller:** conditional fill returns differ from opportunity returns; four correlated views do not increase sample size. **F&O trader/options strategist/volatility trader:** overnight spot/IV moves and option spreads can change price, debit and hedge economics; the owner cannot execute yesterday's mark. **Risk manager/portfolio/head of desk:** maximum loss, funds, margin and drawdown are distinct; only verdict positions may affect hard caps/stops. **Execution/microstructure:** due-mark bid/ask, touch size and all-or-none hedge must be enforced, with no synthetic later fills. **Market research/macro/derivatives flow/equity/investment banking:** news, earnings, RBI/Fed and overnight flows create non-random misses; split outcomes by gap direction. **Regulation:** paper only; exchange and broker margin still constrain a feasible fill. **Behavioural:** avoid pretending a missed limit was a winner or changing limits after seeing morning prices. **Tax/cost accounting:** no-fill pays no trade charges; executed fills include all C1 charges; a debit must be funded. **Data/backtesting engineer/red team:** verify time witness, view isolation, funds recheck and failure replay.
